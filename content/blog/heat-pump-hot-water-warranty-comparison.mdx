A heat pump is a tank plus a small refrigeration unit, and most warranties treat those parts very differently. A "15-year warranty" might mean 15 years on the tank only, parts only, with the last five years pro rata. Here's how to read a heat pump warranty, and what the six models we quote on actually cover. Every warranty below was checked against the manufacturer's own published terms on **28 October 2026**.

## The short answer

- **Look at three numbers, not one:** the tank, the heat pump unit (compressor, fan, controller) and labour.
- **Parts only is not the same as parts and labour.** After the labour period ends, you usually pay for the plumber or technician to fit a replacement part, even if the part itself is free.
- **On published terms, the long tank warranties belong to stainless tanks:** Sanden and Reclaim Energy both offer 15 years, with conditions.
- **For whole-system cover, iStore is the simplest:** 5 years parts and labour on everything, including the anode and relief valve.
- **Some warranties get better with a rebate.** Rheem and Emerald both extend cover where a Solar Victoria rebate has been received, and Rheem also does where VEECs or NSW ESCs were created.
- **Your consumer rights don't end when the warranty does.** More on that below.

## Warranty vs your consumer rights

The ACCC is clear that warranties and consumer guarantees are different things ([ACCC: warranties](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/buying-products-and-services/warranties)):

- **Consumer guarantees are automatic** under the Australian Consumer Law. A product must be of acceptable quality, which includes being safe, durable and free from defects ([ACCC: consumer rights and guarantees](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/buying-products-and-services/consumer-rights-and-guarantees)).
- **A warranty is an extra promise** a manufacturer chooses to make. It can't replace or take away your consumer guarantee rights, and the ACCC says those rights "can last longer than a business's warranty".
- **A warranty can have conditions.** The ACCC gives the example of a warranty that requires yearly servicing: skip it and you may lose the warranty claim, but you still have your consumer guarantee rights.
- **Start with the business that sold it to you.** The ACCC says your first step is to contact the seller, and they can't refuse to help by telling you to contact the manufacturer.

There's no set lifespan in the law. When deciding whether a product was durable enough, the ACCC lists things like what it cost, how similar products last, what maintenance it needed and what the business claimed about it. That's one reason to keep your quote, invoice and service records.

## How to read a heat pump warranty

When you get a quote, check these seven things in the warranty for the exact model number on it:

1. **Tank (cylinder).** Usually the longest number. Stainless and glass-lined (vitreous enamel) tanks have different terms.
2. **Heat pump unit or sealed system.** The compressor, evaporator, fan and refrigeration pipework. This is the expensive bit to fix, and it's often covered for much less time than the tank.
3. **Parts vs labour.** Many warranties drop to parts only after a few years. Reclaim's tank warranties are parts only. Rheem covers cylinder labour for 3 of its 10 cylinder years.
4. **Pro rata years.** Sanden's 15-year stainless tank warranty is 10 years at full replacement and a further 5 years on a pro-rated scale. That means you pay a share in the later years.
5. **Valves and small parts.** Relief valves, tempering valves and anodes often have their own short period, or none.
6. **Conditions.** Licensed or authorised installation, water quality limits, servicing, registration deadlines and time limits for making a claim.
7. **Travel and access.** Sanden and Reclaim both cap service labour or travel, and several brands charge you for remote sites or hard-to-reach installs.

## Warranties that improve with a rebate

Two brands give extra cover where a government rebate or certificate is involved:

- **Rheem** says a **5-year whole-of-product warranty**, including labour, applies where a Solar Victoria hot water rebate was received (installed from 1 July 2023), where Victorian Energy Efficiency Certificates (VEECs) were created (installed from 1 February 2025), or where NSW Energy Savings Certificates (ESCs) were created (installed from 1 December 2025). You need proof of the rebate or certificates, such as the quote showing them, at the service call.
- **Emerald** gives 5 years on the tank, system, ancillary components and labour for goods where a Solar Victoria rebate was received (installed from 1 July 2023), and says you won't pay upfront costs for a successful claim on those goods.

The NSW scheme also sets a floor. The [Hot Water Upgrade Incentive](https://www.energy.nsw.gov.au/households/grants-rebates/household-energy-saving-upgrades/hot-water-upgrade-incentive) page says a heat pump installed with the discount should have a **minimum 5-year warranty against defects** covering the heat pump unit, tank and other components supplied by the manufacturer. Some published warranties above have shorter periods for some parts, so ask your installer to confirm the model on your quote qualifies.

## Conditions that can void a claim

Every warranty we read has conditions. The common ones:

- **Installation by licensed (and sometimes authorised) trades.** Sanden requires installation by a licensed plumber and electrician trained by and working with an authorised Sanden dealer, and only an authorised dealer can do warranty work. iStore requires a 500 kPa pressure reduction valve and says leaving it out voids the warranty.
- **Water quality.** Sanden publishes limits including 600 mg/L total dissolved solids, 200 mg/L hardness and a pH between 6.5 and 8, and excludes faults from unfiltered sources like dams, bores, rainwater and rivers. iStore excludes sediment from unfiltered or bore supplies. Emerald excludes damage from water outside the limits in its installation instructions. On tank or bore water, test it before you choose a model.
- **Maintenance.** Emerald excludes claims where the anode wasn't inspected and replaced as required. Sanden says failing to maintain the system per the installation manual may void the warranty, and its vitreous enamel tank warranty depends on you replacing the anode every 5 years.
- **Third-party add-ons.** Emerald excludes damage from third-party controls or solar diverters it hasn't approved. iStore says third-party controls will void the manufacturer warranty. If you plan to add a diverter or smart switch, ask first.
- **Claim deadlines.** iStore and Sanden both say claims must be made within one month of detecting the defect.
- **Original owner and location.** Sanden's warranty is for the original owner only. iStore, Emerald and Sanden all exclude systems moved from their original installed location.
- **Remote and hard-to-reach sites.** Sanden charges travel beyond metro areas or 25 km from an accredited service agent, and access costs where the unit isn't safely reachable. Emerald may charge reasonable extra travel for remote sites, with notice.

## What the manufacturer's warranty doesn't cover: installation

Manufacturers warrant their product, not the installer's work. Sanden and iStore both say they don't warrant installation work, and Emerald's labour cover excludes the original installation workmanship of an independent installer. If a leak comes from a joint or valve the installer fitted, that's usually the installer's responsibility.

The NSW Government also notes that warranty support may come from the manufacturer rather than the installer for parts, and recommends contacting the manufacturer directly with warranty questions. When you compare quotes, ask each installer:

- What **workmanship warranty** do you give on the installation?
- Who do I call first if something goes wrong: you or the manufacturer?
- Will you **register** the warranty for me, or do I need to?
- Is the warranty conditional on **servicing**, and can you do the service?
- Are there any **travel or access charges** for warranty visits at my address?
