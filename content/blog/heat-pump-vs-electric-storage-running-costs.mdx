An electric storage tank heats water the expensive way: an element turns each kWh of electricity into one kWh of heat. A heat pump moves heat from the air instead, so it needs only a fraction of the electricity for the same hot water. Here's what that's worth, worked out with official tariffs.

## The basic maths

Heating water takes about **2.6 kWh of heat for every 50 L** raised by 45 °C (for example, from 15 °C to 60 °C).

- An **electric element** turns electricity into heat one for one, so it uses about the same in kWh. The tank also loses heat while it stands, which we leave out (that flatters the electric tank).
- A **heat pump** divides the heat by its COP (coefficient of performance). We use a cautious seasonal **COP of 3.5**. The [models in our range](/models) are rated higher at their test conditions, and Stiebel Eltron quotes a seasonal COP of 3.58 for the [WWK 302 H](/models/stiebel-eltron-wwk-302).

## Savings by household usage

| Hot water heated per day | Electric tank | Heat pump (COP 3.5) | Electricity saved a year |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 100 L | 5.2 kWh | 1.5 kWh | about 1,360 kWh |
| 150 L | 7.9 kWh | 2.2 kWh | about 2,050 kWh |
| 200 L | 10.5 kWh | 3.0 kWh | about 2,730 kWh |
| 250 L | 13.1 kWh | 3.7 kWh | about 3,410 kWh |

## What that's worth: an official tariff example

Tariffs differ by state and retailer, so we use one official, published set as an example: the Queensland Competition Authority's 2026–27 notified prices for regional Queensland ([Queensland Government Gazette, 5 June 2026](http://www.qca.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/050626-29-extra-gazette.pdf)). The gazette prices exclude GST; the GST-inclusive rates below are our calculation (× 1.1).

| Hot water heated per day | Tariff 11, general (about 28.9c/kWh) | Tariff 33, controlled load (about 16.8c/kWh) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 100 L | about $394 a year | about $229 a year |
| 150 L | about $591 a year | about $343 a year |
| 200 L | about $788 a year | about $457 a year |
| 250 L | about $985 a year | about $572 a year |

Outside regional Queensland, use the hot water rate on your own bill: multiply the "electricity saved" figure by your rate in $/kWh. If you have rooftop solar, a heat pump on a midday timer can run mostly on your own power, and the real cost is the feed-in tariff you'd otherwise have earned.

## Controlled-load and off-peak tariffs

Many electric tanks run on a cheaper controlled-load tariff that only supplies power for part of the day. In the QCA's schedule, Tariff 31 supplies power for at least 8 hours a day and Tariff 33 for at least 16 hours, on the Ergon Energy network. A heat pump heats more slowly than a big element, so ask your installer whether your model suits the tariff you're on, or whether a timer on your general tariff or solar is the better choice.

## Rebates for replacing an electric tank

Official figures, last checked 16 October 2026:

- **Federal STCs**, Australia-wide: about **12–18 STCs** for our six models in 2026, worth up to **$480–$720** at the $40 Clearing House cap ([Clean Energy Regulator](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-renewable-energy-systems/solar-water-heaters-and-air-source-heat-pumps)). See [how STCs work when you replace your hot water](/blog/how-stcs-work-when-you-replace-your-hot-water).
- **NSW:** the [Hot Water Upgrade Incentive](https://www.energy.nsw.gov.au/households/grants-rebates/household-energy-saving-upgrades/hot-water-upgrade-incentive) lists an indicative discount of **up to $640** when replacing an electric water heater with a heat pump.
- **Victoria:** [Solar Victoria's hot water rebate](https://www.solar.vic.gov.au/hot-water-rebate) is **up to $1,000** (up to $1,400 for locally made products) for eligible households.
