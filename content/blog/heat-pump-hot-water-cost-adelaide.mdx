## Adelaide guide prices before and after STCs

These figures are **supply and standard installation before discounts**, then after **zone 3** STCs. On the Clean Energy Regulator's postcode map for air source heat pumps, the whole 5000–5214 range is zone 3. That covers the CBD, the northern, southern and western suburbs and the Adelaide Hills. Mount Barker (5251) is zone 3 too. STC values use the Clearing House cap of **$40 excluding GST** per certificate ([CER buy and sell STCs page](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-technology-certificates/buy-and-sell-small-scale-technology-certificates), last updated 14 September 2026). For a 2026 install, each model's 10-year register figure is halved and rounded down. Guide prices and STC counts come from our model data and are provisional until a partner installer quotes your home.

**Outside the metro area:** Kangaroo Island (5220–5223) and much of the Limestone Coast, including Mount Gambier (5271–5291), are in **zone 4**, which earns a few more certificates. Check your own postcode in our [rebate checker](/rebates).

Air-source heat pumps under **425 L** can create STCs, and if you claim them yourself you have **12 months** from installation ([CER solar water heaters and air source heat pumps](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-renewable-energy-systems/solar-water-heaters-and-air-source-heat-pumps), last updated 29 May 2025). Most installers take the certificates and knock the value off the quote. Open-market STC prices are often a little under the $40 cap, so the STC line on your quote may be slightly less than the table.

## South Australia's REPS hot water discount

South Australia doesn't have a fixed-dollar hot water rebate like Victoria or NSW. The state program is the **Retailer Energy Productivity Scheme (REPS)**. Energy retailers have yearly targets and meet them by delivering approved activities in homes and businesses, either directly or through contracted activity providers ([Department for Energy and Mining REPS page](https://www.energymining.sa.gov.au/industry/energy-efficiency-and-productivity/retailer-energy-productivity-scheme-reps), checked 10 November 2026). Targets for the **2026–2030** stage were published in the SA Government Gazette on 24 December 2025, and ESCOSA expects REPS to run until at least **31 December 2030**, subject to future government decisions.

### What REPS offers for hot water

ESCOSA's [water heating activity page](https://www.escosa.sa.gov.au/industry/reps/residential-activities/activity-water-heating-residential) (checked 10 November 2026) lists four household activities. Two matter most for a heat pump:

- **WH1, replace or upgrade your hot water system.** A heat pump is one of the allowed replacement types.
- **WH4, connect a new or existing heat pump to an approved demand response aggregator.** Your heat pump's run times can then be adjusted to help manage demand on the grid.

The others are WH2 (efficient showerhead) and WH3 (moving an electric water heater to an eligible tariff with solar sponge and off-peak blocks).

The official [WH1 activity specification](https://www.energymining.sa.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0005/1375763/Replace-or-Upgrade-Water-Heater-WH1.pdf) (version WH1.2026.1, checked 10 November 2026) adds some useful detail:

- It covers established **class 1 homes** (houses and townhouses) and **class 2** dwellings (apartments). Water heaters installed as part of building a **new house** are excluded.
- A heat pump installed from 1 January 2026 must earn at least **13 STCs in zone 3**. On our model data, all six models above earn 14 to 16 in zone 3. Confirm the exact model with your provider.
- The old water heater must be removed from the property.
- The scheme credits a heat pump with more energy savings in a home **not connected to mains gas** (a factor of 113) than one that is connected (33). Retailers chase these credits, so a heat pump replacing an electric tank in a home without gas may attract a bigger discount. That last part is our reading of the specification. No discount amount is published.

### How the discount works

From [ESCOSA's REPS FAQs for households](https://www.escosa.sa.gov.au/industry/reps/frequently-asked-questions/faqs-residential) (checked 10 November 2026):

- **It isn't a cash rebate.** The SA Government doesn't pay you. The discount is built into the price, through discounted products or installation, and applied when the job is done.
- **No set price.** ESCOSA doesn't set or publish prices for REPS activities. Costs vary by activity, provider and location.
- **Co-payment.** Where one applies, the minimum is **$33 including GST**. That minimum doesn't apply to eligible priority group or concession households.
- **You don't need to be the retailer's customer** to access its offers ([Energy & Mining energy saving rebates page](https://www.energymining.sa.gov.au/consumers/energy-advisory-service/energy-saving-rebates), checked 10 November 2026).
- **Usually once per address.** Each water heating activity can generally be done once per property.
- **Priority group** households (for example concession card holders, or renters paying $500 a week or less) may get further discounts or offers.
- **Voluntary.** You can compare REPS offers with quotes from installers outside the scheme, and ESCOSA notes non-REPS services may sometimes be available sooner or at a lower cost.

To use WH1, you contact an activity provider who offers it. ESCOSA's water heating page lists those providers (five offered WH1 when we checked). They confirm your eligibility and tell you any cost. Under the REPS Code, you must be told any co-payment at booking and the **final total cost** before work starts.

### Doorknockers and REPS

Some REPS offers arrive at the door. Anyone delivering a REPS activity must wear photo ID showing their name, company (with ABN) and phone number. They must give you a REPS information statement, and they must leave when asked. A provider can only take a deposit if the job will be done within the same calendar year (or within 30 business days for bookings in November or December), at the quoted price. Read our [red flags in "free" hot water offers](/blog/free-heat-pump-hot-water-red-flags) before you sign anything on the doorstep.

### Any other SA rebates for hot water?

On the SA Government consumer energy pages we checked today, the only state incentive for household hot water is REPS. We found no separate SA cash rebate for heat pump hot water. ESCOSA also confirms solar panels and batteries aren't offered under REPS. So for most Adelaide homes, the stack is **federal STCs plus whatever REPS discount a provider offers**.

## Adelaide summers, Hills winters and model choice

The Bureau of Meteorology's West Terrace station averages a **July minimum of about 7.6 °C** with no frost days, and a **January mean maximum of about 28.7 °C**. City winters sit comfortably inside every model's rated range. Two local things to plan for:

- **Summer heat.** Rated ranges for our models top out at about **42–43 °C**. On extreme days, a unit may lean on its backup element if it has one. Putting the unit in shade, away from a hot west-facing wall, helps.
- **The Adelaide Hills.** The Hills are colder than the city. A model rated to lower temperatures, like the Reclaim or Sanden CO2 systems (rated to **−10 °C**), keeps heating strongly on frosty mornings. The iStore and Rheem are rated from **−5 °C**.

Read [the best heat pump for cold climates](/blog/best-heat-pump-cold-climate) and [do heat pumps work in winter](/blog/do-heat-pumps-work-in-winter-australia), or see our [Adelaide heat pump page](/heat-pump-hot-water/adelaide) for local picks.

## What drives an Adelaide quote up or down

Guide prices assume a simple swap. On real Adelaide properties, these are the usual movers:

- **Switching from gas.** A gas-to-heat-pump job usually needs a new electrical circuit and the old gas line capped. A good quote lists both.
- **Electrical work.** A dedicated circuit, isolator, RCD or switchboard upgrade adds cost, especially in older villas and cottages.
- **Access.** Narrow side paths, sloping Hills blocks and battle-axe blocks can mean extra labour.
- **Noise and neighbours.** Close boundaries mean the outdoor unit may sit near a bedroom window. Read [heat pump noise and placement](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-noise-and-placement). A split system can help where space is tight; see [split vs integrated](/blog/split-vs-integrated-heat-pump).
- **Pad or stand.** A level concrete pad or wall bracket may be needed.
- **Tank size.** Couples often suit **220–270 L**; bigger families more often look at **315 L**. Use [what size heat pump do I need](/blog/what-size-heat-pump-do-i-need).

1. Check your postcode's STC zone in the [rebate checker](/rebates).
