Short answer: **yes, you can usually still go to a heat pump when your hot water dies, and you don't always lose the rebates by moving fast.** Victoria has a specific emergency installation rule, NSW needs one form signed before work starts, and federal STCs work the same as any other job. Here's what to do first and how each discount works when you can't wait, checked against official government pages on **31 October 2026**.

## First, make it safe

- **If water is leaking from the tank**, turn off the cold water isolation valve at the tank and switch off the hot water circuit at the switchboard (or the gas at the appliance if it's a gas unit).
- **If water is running from the relief valve drain** and doesn't stop, ask a plumber to check it.
- **If you smell gas**, leave the area and call your gas distributor's emergency line.
- **Don't try to fix it yourself.** Plumbing and electrical work on water heaters must be done by licensed tradies. See [who can install a heat pump](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-installation-requirements).

Then work out whether it's a repair or a replacement. A plumber can often replace a failed element, thermostat or valve. A leaking tank generally means replacement. If your system is near the end of its life anyway, see [how long hot water systems last](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-lifespan-maintenance).

## Can a heat pump go in as fast as a like-for-like swap?

Often, yes. An integrated heat pump (the tank and heat pump in one unit) usually goes where your old tank was, on a standard cold and hot water connection and an electrical circuit. The things that can slow it down are:

- **Stock.** Ask the installer which models they have on hand today, and be open to a second choice if your first isn't available.
- **Space and noise.** A heat pump needs airflow and shouldn't sit right next to a bedroom window. See [noise and placement](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-noise-and-placement).
- **Switching from gas.** You'll need the gas capped and an electrical circuit run, which can add time.
- **Indoor tanks.** Most integrated heat pumps are designed to go outdoors, so a tank in a cupboard may need a split system or relocation.

For timing on a normal job, see [how long a heat pump installation takes](/blog/how-long-does-heat-pump-installation-take).

## Rebates in an emergency, by scheme

### Federal STCs (all states)

Small-scale technology certificates (STCs) apply whether or not the job is urgent. Most installers take them off the price at the point of sale. If you create them yourself instead, the Clean Energy Regulator says you must do it within 12 months of installation, and you can't if you've already assigned them to an agent. Air source heat pumps must have a tank under 425 L ([CER: solar water heaters and air source heat pumps](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-renewable-energy-systems/solar-water-heaters-and-air-source-heat-pumps)). The STC Clearing House price is capped at **$40 excluding GST** ([CER](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-technology-certificates/buy-and-sell-small-scale-technology-certificates), updated 14 September 2026). See [how STCs work](/blog/how-stcs-work-when-you-replace-your-hot-water).

### Victoria: Solar Victoria emergency installations

The [Solar Victoria hot water rebate](https://www.solar.vic.gov.au/hot-water-rebate) is up to $1,000, or up to $1,400 for eligible locally made products. Normally Solar Victoria recommends you don't accept a quote in writing or pay a deposit before it confirms you're eligible. But it has a specific rule for breakdowns:

- If your hot water system breaks down or is unsafe, **you don't have to wait for Solar Victoria to confirm you're eligible** before installing a replacement.
- You must prove you're eligible **as soon as possible after installation**. If you don't meet the criteria, you may not get a rebate.
- Use an **authorised retailer**, tell them you intend to apply for the rebate, and choose an **eligible product**.
- Have ID and proof of income ready, apply online, and **tick the emergency installations box** when you enter your quote details.

The usual criteria still apply: owner-occupier, combined household taxable income under $150,000, property under $3 million, and an existing system at least 3 years old from the date of purchase (page updated 20 October 2026). See our [Solar Victoria rebate guide](/blog/solar-victoria-hot-water-rebate).

If your gas hot water has failed, note that Solar Victoria says existing Victorian homes must upgrade gas hot water to an efficient electric alternative, like a heat pump, as it reaches end of life **from 1 March 2027**.

### NSW: sign the nomination form before work starts

The NSW [Hot Water Upgrade Incentive](https://www.energy.nsw.gov.au/households/grants-rebates/household-energy-saving-upgrades/hot-water-upgrade-incentive) gives indicative discounts of up to $640 when you replace an electric water heater with a heat pump, or up to $330 when you replace gas (page updated 6 February 2026). The key rule for an urgent job: the installer's **nomination form must be signed and returned before installation begins**, because you won't be reimbursed for work already completed. So even if the plumber can come today, sign the form and get the quote showing the discount before they start. The heat pump also needs a minimum 5-year warranty against defects covering the heat pump unit, tank and other manufacturer components.

### Queensland and other states

Queensland has no general household heat pump rebate, so STCs are the main discount. See [Queensland heat pump rebates](/blog/queensland-heat-pump-rebate-2026). Replacing a water heater, including a heat pump, is notifiable work, and the licensed plumber must register it with the QBCC within 10 business days of issuing the invoice ([QBCC: what is notifiable work](https://qbcc.qld.gov.au/worksite-building-practice/plumbing-work/what-notifiable-work)). For other states, check our [rebates by state guide](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-rebates-by-state).

## Don't let urgency cost you

When your shower's cold it's tempting to say yes to the first offer. A few protections and checks:

- **You called them, so get it in writing.** Ask for a written quote with the exact model, the installed price and each discount on a separate line.
- **If someone approached you uninvited**, the Australian Consumer Law gives you a 10 business day cooling-off period. During it, the seller can't supply services or take payment, except for electricity, gas or emergency repairs ([ACCC: telemarketing and door-to-door sales](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/buying-products-and-services/telemarketing-and-door-to-door-sales)). See our [red flags in "free" hot water offers](/blog/free-heat-pump-hot-water-red-flags).
- **Check the size.** A rushed swap is a common time to end up with the wrong tank. See [what size heat pump you need](/blog/what-size-heat-pump-do-i-need).
- **Check the warranty.** See our [warranty comparison](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-warranty-comparison).
- **Keep the paperwork**: invoice, STC assignment form, electrical safety certificate, plumbing compliance record and warranty registration.

## Questions to ask on the phone

- Which heat pump models do you have in stock, and when can you install?
- What's the total price after STCs and any state discount?
- Do I need to sign anything before you start (NSW nomination form, Solar Victoria quote)?
- Will you remove and dispose of the old system?
- Who does the plumbing and electrical work, and what are their licence numbers?
- If I'm on gas, what's involved in capping it and running power?
