If you're in **Queensland** and hoping for a state rebate on a heat pump hot water system, the short answer is that the big one has gone. The **Climate Smart Energy Savers** rebate, which paid up to **$1,000** for hot water, has finished. What's left is the federal **STC** discount, cheaper running costs on an **economy tariff**, **low-rate green loans**, and two programs that only help **landlords** and **community housing providers**. Here's each one, using only official pages checked on **22 October 2026**.

## The short answer

- **Climate Smart Energy Savers:** finished. It paid **$1,000** (lower incomes) or **$800** for a heat pump or solar hot water system, and its regulation set **30 June 2025** as the latest closing date ([Queensland legislation](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/asmade/sl-2023-0119)).
- **No current household hot water rebate** was listed on the Queensland Government's [home energy savings page](https://www.qld.gov.au/housing/home-energy-savings) when we checked on 22 October 2026.
- **Federal STCs** still apply: about **14 to 16** in south-east Queensland and **12 to 14** in the north for our six models, worth up to **$480 to $640**.
- **Tariff 31 and 33** can cut what you pay to run the heat pump ([qld.gov.au](https://www.qld.gov.au/housing/buying-owning-home/energy-water-home/electricity/reduce-electricity-bill)).
- **Community housing providers** can get up to **$4,500 ex GST per dwelling** until applications close on **30 November 2026** ([Business Queensland](https://www.business.qld.gov.au/industries/housing-accommodation/community/energy-upgrades/provider-owned-properties)).

## What happened to the Climate Smart Energy Savers rebate

The scheme was a Queensland Government rebate for buying energy efficient appliances, and a **heat pump or solar hot water system** was one of the eligible items. Under the [2023 regulation](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/asmade/sl-2023-0119) that set out the scheme, it paid:

| Household | Hot water rebate |
| --- | --- |
| Combined taxable income of **$66,667 or less** | **$1,000** |
| Other eligible households | **$800** |

The same regulation says the scheme closed on the earliest of three dates: the closing day announced on the department's website, the day the money ran out, or **30 June 2025**. So even on the latest possible date, it's been closed for well over a year. If an installer or a website still quotes a "$1,000 Queensland rebate", ask them to show you where it's on an official qld.gov.au page today.

One useful detail from the old rules: the rebate could be claimed on top of federal STCs. The STCs are still here, and they're now the main discount.

## Federal STCs: the discount that's still here

STCs (small-scale technology certificates) come from the federal Small-scale Renewable Energy Scheme and work everywhere in Australia. To qualify, the heat pump must be on the [Clean Energy Regulator's register](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-renewable-energy-systems/solar-water-heaters-and-air-source-heat-pumps) and hold **less than 425 L**. Most installers take the value off your quote up front, and if you claim them yourself you have **12 months** from installation.

How many you get depends on your postcode's heat pump zone. Most of south-east Queensland is in **zone 3**, while Townsville and Cairns are in **zone 1**. Using the register counts on our model pages and the 2026 factor of one half:

STC values use the **$40 excluding GST** price cap in the STC Clearing House ([CER](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-technology-certificates/buy-and-sell-small-scale-technology-certificates), page updated 14 September 2026). Installers usually sell on the open market for a bit less, so your real STC discount may be lower. Guide prices are supply and standard installation, before rebates, and are provisional until a partner installer quotes your home.

Zones don't follow city lines neatly. Parts of western and central Queensland are in zones 1 and 2, so check your own postcode in our [rebate checker](/rebates). More detail: [how STCs work when you replace your hot water](/blog/how-stcs-work-when-you-replace-your-hot-water).

### STCs shrink every January

The number of STCs is based on the years left until the scheme ends in **2030**, so it drops each year. On our model data, a 2027 install earns about a fifth fewer certificates than the same install in 2026. If you're already planning to switch, installing before the end of the year keeps the larger discount.

## Tariff 31 and 33: cheaper to run

With no state rebate, the next biggest saving is on your power bill. The Queensland Government's [electricity savings page](https://www.qld.gov.au/housing/buying-owning-home/energy-water-home/electricity/reduce-electricity-bill) (last reviewed 24 March 2026) explains the two economy tariffs on the Energex and Ergon networks:

- **Economy Tariff 31** is for hot water systems and is available for **at least 8 hours a day**.
- **Economy Tariff 33** is available for **at least 18 hours a day**, and rewards you for using power outside peak times (usually **4pm to 9pm**).

The page also warns that moving an appliance onto an economy tariff for the first time means paying an electrician to install a new meter, so factor that into your quote. A heat pump on Tariff 31 has a shorter window to reheat the tank, so ask your installer which tariff suits the model and your household. We've done the running cost maths at Queensland's 2026–27 regulated prices in [is a heat pump worth it in Queensland?](/blog/is-a-heat-pump-worth-it-in-queensland). If you've got rooftop solar, running the heat pump on a midday timer can cost less again.

## Low-rate green loans (federal)

Queensland doesn't have a state loan scheme like the ACT's, but the federal **Clean Energy Finance Corporation** backs discounted green finance through its [Household Energy Upgrades Fund](https://www.cefc.com.au/where-we-invest/special-investment-programs/household-energy-upgrades-fund/). On 22 October 2026 the CEFC page listed products including:

- **Brighte** discounted green loans of **$2,000 to $55,000**, with discounted introductory rates
- **Plenti** discounted green loans
- **CommBank**, **ING** and **Westpac** home energy upgrade loans or mortgage top-ups (ING and Westpac up to **$50,000**)

The CEFC lists **heat pumps** and **solar hot water** among the technologies that may be eligible, but it doesn't lend to you directly and each lender decides what it'll finance. Check with the lender that a heat pump water heater qualifies and compare the rate with other options before you sign. This is general information, not financial advice.

## Help for landlords and community housing

### Community housing providers: up to $4,500 per dwelling

The **Queensland Community Housing Energy Upgrades** program is the one Queensland rebate that still covers heat pump hot water. Per the [Business Queensland page](https://www.business.qld.gov.au/industries/housing-accommodation/community/energy-upgrades/provider-owned-properties) (updated 14 October 2026):

- It's for **registered community housing providers** that own and manage their dwellings, not private households.
- Rebates are **up to $4,500 excluding GST per dwelling**, from an **$18 million** pool, and you can only apply once per dwelling.
- **Heat pump and solar hot water systems** are eligible, including shared systems that serve two or more dwellings.
- Applications close on **30 November 2026**, and installations must be finished by **30 June 2027**.

### Landlords: Supercharged Solar for Renters is for solar, not hot water

The [Supercharged Solar for Renters](https://www.qld.gov.au/housing/home-energy-savings/supercharged-solar-for-renters) program, open since 12 December 2025, gives eligible landlords rebates of **up to $3,500** to install **rooftop solar** on rental properties. It doesn't pay for a heat pump. A landlord could pair the two, though, since a heat pump on a midday timer makes good use of daytime solar.

### Pensioners and seniors: help with the bill, not the system

Eligible Queensland pensioners, veterans and seniors can get the **Electricity Rebate of $399.47 a year** (GST inclusive) on their power bill ([qld.gov.au](https://www.qld.gov.au/community/cost-of-living-support/concessions/energy-concessions/electricity-gas-rebates), updated 10 July 2026). It doesn't pay for a new hot water system, but it's worth claiming if you're eligible.

## Is it still worth switching without a state rebate?

For most Queensland homes replacing an electric storage tank, yes. Warm winters suit heat pumps, and the running cost saving does most of the work. In our [Queensland running cost guide](/blog/is-a-heat-pump-worth-it-in-queensland), a heat pump saves about **$340 a year** on a controlled-load tariff, or about **$590** on the general tariff, for a household using 150 L a day. Losing the old $800 to $1,000 rebate adds roughly two to three years to the payback on a controlled-load tariff, or under two years on the general tariff.

It's less clear-cut if you use very little hot water, already have solar hot water that works well, or are happy with a cheap gas system. Our [heat pump vs gas running costs](/blog/heat-pump-vs-gas-hot-water-running-costs) guide covers that case.

## If your hot water has just died

You don't need to wait for any pre-approval in Queensland, because the only discount left for households is STCs, and most installers apply them at the point of sale. Just make sure the quote lists the **STCs as a separate line** and names the exact model, so you can check it's on the CER register. If you need hot water fast, see our [broken hot water page](/hot-water-broken).

## Questions to ask your installer

- What's the exact **model number**, and is it on the **CER register**?
- How many **STCs** does it create in my postcode, and what price are you using for them?
- Does it suit **Tariff 31 or 33**, and does the quote include any **new meter** or switchboard work?
- Does the price include **removing the old tank** and any **plumbing changes**?
- What's the **warranty** on the heat pump unit and the tank?
- If you mention a "Queensland rebate", **which official program** is it?
