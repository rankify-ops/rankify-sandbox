## Who fixes a broken hot water system in a rental?

The landlord, in almost every case. Each of the three biggest states lists a hot water failure as an urgent or emergency repair:

- **Victoria.** Consumer Affairs Victoria lists "an essential service or appliance for hot water ... is not working" as an urgent repair, and rental providers must make urgent repairs immediately. If the rental provider or agent doesn't respond, the renter can organise and pay for the repair if it costs no more than $2,500, and the rental provider must pay them back within 7 days ([CAV: repairs in rental properties](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/repairs-alterations-safety-and-pets/repairs/repairs-in-rental-properties)).
- **NSW.** "A failure or breakdown of any essential service for hot water" is an urgent repair. The tenant must tell the landlord or agent straight away, and if it isn't fixed, follow the tenancy agreement's approved repairer steps ([NSW Government: urgent repairs](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/rules/urgent-repairs-residential-rental-properties)).
- **Queensland.** "A failure or breakdown of an essential service or appliance on the premises for hot water" is an emergency repair. If the tenant can't reach the emergency contact in a reasonable time, either the tenant or the property manager can arrange repairs up to 4 weeks' rent, and a tenant who pays must be reimbursed within a minimum of 7 days of a written request ([RTA: emergency repairs](https://www.rta.qld.gov.au/during-a-tenancy/repairs/emergency-repairs)).

**Renters:** call the emergency number in your tenancy agreement first, then confirm it in writing by email or text. Don't book a replacement yourself unless your state's rules allow it and you've tried to reach the landlord or agent.

## Victoria: the 1 March 2027 rental hot water rule

Consumer Affairs Victoria's new minimum energy efficiency standards for rentals start in phases from **1 March 2027** ([CAV: new minimum energy efficiency standards](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/resources-and-tools/legislation/public-consultations-and-reviews/new-minimum-energy-efficiency-standards)). For hot water:

- The trigger is when the existing hot water system **permanently fails and can't be repaired**. A system in good working order doesn't need to be replaced.
- The replacement must be an **efficient heat pump water heater** or an **electric-boosted solar water heater**. CAV's guide for rental providers adds that gas systems can't be replaced with another gas system after 1 March 2027.
- **Exemptions** may apply, for example where hot water comes from a centralised building system, installation isn't allowed under heritage laws or owners corporation rules, or there isn't enough space for a heat pump or rooftop solar panels. Keep proof if an exemption applies.
- Separately, from 13 October 2026 all Victorian rental providers must keep records showing the property meets the rental minimum standards ([CAV: minimum standards](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/repairs-alterations-safety-and-pets/minimum-standards/minimum-standards-for-rental-properties)). Keep the invoice, model details and compliance certificate from your heat pump install.

If you own a Victorian rental with an old gas or electric tank, it's worth planning now. Choosing a heat pump before the old tank dies means you can compare quotes, rather than taking whatever's available on the day the renter has no hot water.

## Tax: repair or capital?

The ATO says replacing an **entire unit**, including a depreciating asset, isn't an immediate repair deduction. It's capital in nature and claimed over several years ([ATO: repair and maintenance expenses](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/property-and-land/residential-rental-properties/rental-expenses/repair-and-maintenance-expenses)). On the ATO's list of rental property items, hot water systems are depreciating assets and hot water piping is capital works ([ATO: residential rental property items](https://www.ato.gov.au/forms-and-instructions/rental-properties-2018/residential-rental-property-assets/residential-rental-property-items)). Repairing part of a system, like a valve or element, is a different question. Ask your accountant how it applies to you; this isn't tax advice.

## Why a heat pump makes sense for a rental

- **Renters usually pay the power bill**, so lower running costs make the property easier to lease and keep tenants happy. See our [running cost comparison](/blog/heat-pump-vs-electric-storage-running-costs).
- **It avoids a second replacement** in Victoria, where a failed gas or inefficient electric system can't be replaced like for like from 1 March 2027.
- **Discounts lower the upfront cost**: STCs everywhere, plus VEU in Victoria and possibly the NSW incentive.
- **Fewer callouts** if you choose a model with a solid warranty and a sensible size. See [what size heat pump you need](/blog/what-size-heat-pump-do-i-need) and [noise and placement](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-noise-and-placement), since a heat pump outside a bedroom window can cause complaints.

## Checklist for landlords and property managers

- Record the age, type and model of the current hot water system before it fails.
- Line up a licensed installer in advance so an urgent breakdown doesn't leave renters waiting.
- Check whether an apartment's owners corporation rules or the building's hot water setup limit your options.
- In NSW, have the nomination form signed before installation begins, even on an urgent job.
- Give renters the operating instructions and the warranty contact.
- Keep the invoice, STC paperwork, compliance certificate and warranty registration for tax and, in Victoria, for minimum standards records.
