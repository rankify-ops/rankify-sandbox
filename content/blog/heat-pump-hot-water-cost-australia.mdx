## What STCs take off in 2026

Federal STCs apply Australia-wide. The Clean Energy Regulator (CER) method is: take the model's certificate count for your postcode zone from the CER register, then multiply it by the factor for the installation year (**0.5 for 2026**), rounding down.

For our six models, that's about **12–18 STCs** in 2026 depending on the model and your postcode zone. At the **$40 Clearing House cap** (excluding GST), that's worth **up to $480–$720**. Installers usually sell STCs at the open-market price, which is normally lower, so treat these as maximums. Our [rebate checker](/rebates) works it out for your postcode.

## Worked examples (illustrative)

These use our guide prices, the maximum STC value at the $40 cap, and official state amounts. Your real figure depends on your site, eligibility and the STC price your installer gets.

- **Melbourne (3000), iStore 270L:** $3,590 guide price, minus up to $640 in STCs (16 certificates, zone 4), minus up to $1,000 Solar Victoria rebate if you're eligible. That's **from about $1,950**.
- **Sydney (2000), iStore 270L replacing an electric tank:** $3,590, minus up to $600 in STCs (15, zone 3), minus up to $640 NSW Hot Water Upgrade Incentive (indicative). That's **from about $2,350**.
- **Brisbane (4000), iStore 270L:** $3,590, minus up to $600 in STCs (15, zone 3). We couldn't find a current Queensland state rebate. That's **from about $2,990**.

Victoria's rebate has eligibility rules (owner-occupier, income and property value caps, system age) and is 50% of the purchase price up to $1,000. NSW's amounts are indicative. Your installer confirms what can be combined. See [rebates by state](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-rebates-by-state).

## What changes the price

- **Tank size and type.** Bigger tanks and CO2 split systems cost more.
- **Site work.** Relocating the unit, a new base, extra pipework or tricky access.
- **Electrical.** Older switchboards sometimes need an upgrade.
- **Timing.** STCs are worth less each year until 2030, as the deeming period shrinks.

## Running costs

A heat pump uses far less electricity than a conventional electric storage tank to heat the same water. What you save depends on your tariff, household size and climate. If you have rooftop solar, a midday timer helps you use your own power. Our [heat pump vs electric guide](/blog/heat-pump-vs-electric-hot-water) explains why.
