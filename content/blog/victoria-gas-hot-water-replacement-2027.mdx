Short answer: **from 1 March 2027, when a mains-gas hot water system in an existing Victorian home reaches end of life and needs replacing, it generally can't be swapped for another gas unit — the replacement has to be electric, typically a heat pump.** Working gas cooktops and space heaters in existing owner-occupier homes are not forced out. Here's what changes, who it hits, the official exemptions, and the rebates that soften the bill, checked against Victorian and federal pages on **6 November 2026**.

## What changes on 1 January 2027 vs 1 March 2027

Victoria is rolling out building electrification and rental energy-efficiency standards in phases ([Energy Victoria: new electrification and efficiency standards](https://www.energy.vic.gov.au/households/electric-and-efficiency-standards-for-buildings), page last updated 9 October 2026):

| Date | What starts | Who it mainly affects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1 January 2027** | New homes and most new commercial buildings must be built all-electric | New home buyers, builders, developers, building surveyors |
| **1 March 2027** | End-of-life gas water heaters in **existing** residential buildings must be replaced with electric alternatives (Plumbing Regulations pathway) | Owner-occupiers, plumbers, electricians |
| **1 March 2027** | Rental minimum energy efficiency standards begin in phases, including the hot water trigger | Rental providers, renters, agents |

[Victoria's Gas Substitution Roadmap](https://www.planning.vic.gov.au/guides-and-resources/strategies-and-initiatives/victorias-gas-substitution-roadmap) (last updated 12 August 2026) puts the same split in plain terms: all-electric new builds from 1 January 2027; existing-home gas hot water replaced with electric at end of life from 1 March 2027; **no change forced on existing gas cooking or space heating** in existing residential buildings.

The legal hook for existing homes is Division 3 of Part 6A of the Plumbing Regulations 2018, inserted by the [Building and Plumbing Amendment Regulations 2025 (S.R. No. 140/2025)](https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au/as-made/statutory-rules/building-and-plumbing-amendment-regulations-2025). Regulation **45D** holds the water-heater rules back until **1 March 2027**. Regulation **45E** then stops plumbing work that installs a reticulated (mains) gas water heater, or replaces one with another reticulated gas water heater, for an existing Class 1 or Class 2 building (and associated swimming pools), unless an exemption applies.

## Owner-occupiers: gas hot water at end of life

For people who own and live in their home, Energy Victoria summarises it this way: from **1 March 2027**, if a gas hot water appliance **breaks and cannot be repaired**, it must be replaced with an **electric alternative**. Support listed on that page includes Victorian Energy Upgrades (VEU) discounts and the Solar Homes hot water rebate if you're eligible.

Important clarifiers from the official pages:

- **Repair is still allowed.** The rule bites on installation and like-for-like gas *replacement*, not on fixing a thermocouple, valve or burner on the existing heater.
- **You don't have to replace a working system early.** End of life means the unit needs replacing, not that every gas tank must go on 1 March.
- **Gas cooktops and space heating stay.** Planning Victoria and Engage Victoria both say there will be **no change** to existing gas cooking or space heating appliances in existing homes. You're encouraged to switch later for bill savings, but 1 March 2027 does not force those upgrades for owner-occupiers.
- **Pools count too.** The VEU hot water page and regulation 45E both cover gas swimming pool heaters appurtenant to Class 1 or Class 2 buildings.

Victorian Energy Upgrades puts the homeowner rule in one sentence: from 1 March 2027, if a gas water heater (including a gas swimming pool heater) needs replacing in an existing Victorian home, it **cannot** be replaced with another mains-gas water heater and must be replaced with an electric system ([VEU hot water system discounts](https://www.energy.vic.gov.au/victorian-energy-upgrades/products/hot-water-system-discounts), page last updated 26 October 2026).

## Renters and landlords: the separate rental standard

Rental properties sit under Consumer Affairs Victoria's [new minimum energy efficiency standards](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/resources-and-tools/legislation/public-consultations-and-reviews/new-minimum-energy-efficiency-standards) (last updated 21 October 2026), which phase in from 1 March 2027.

For hot water specifically:

- The trigger is when the existing system **permanently fails and can't be repaired**.
- The replacement must be an **efficient heat pump water heater** or an **electric-boosted solar water heater**.
- A system in good working order does **not** need early replacement.
- **Exemptions** may apply where hot water comes from a **centralised building system**, installation isn't allowed under **heritage laws or owners corporation rules**, or there isn't **enough space** for a heat pump or rooftop solar panels. Keep proof if an exemption applies.

Energy Victoria's rental snapshot matches that wording and notes rental providers can claim a VEU discount of up to **$560** when replacing gas hot water with a heat pump ([rental homes page](https://www.energy.vic.gov.au/households/electric-and-efficiency-standards-for-buildings/energy-efficiency-for-rental-properties-in-victoria), page last updated 9 October 2026). Solar Victoria's hot water rebate is **owner-occupier only**, so landlords lean on STCs and VEU instead. More detail for landlords and renters is in our [rental hot water guide](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-rental-property).

## Official exemptions (existing homes)

Energy Victoria and Planning Victoria both say exemptions exist where replacing gas hot water with electric is impractical or presents challenges. The detailed list for existing Class 1 and Class 2 buildings is in **regulation 45E** of the Plumbing Regulations as amended.

**Exemptions that can apply to installing or replacing:**

- **Not enough space** indoors, and no suitable outdoor space (the regulations list factors such as ventilation, roof or balcony load, noise near bedrooms and neighbours, and access for maintenance).
- **Centralised gas system** supplying a Class 2 building, or a pool served that way.
- An electric option that **another Act, regulation or law** makes unlawful (heritage and similar controls sit here).
- Going electric would need an alteration to a **main switchboard or distribution board**.
- Going electric would need **network augmentation** beyond a basic or standard connection.

**Extra exemptions that apply to replacement only:**

- Temporarily disconnecting and reconnecting the existing gas unit.
- Replacing a reticulated gas water heater that **does not require a connection to an electricity network service** to operate, with another type of reticulated gas water heater. Distributor guidance (for example AusNet's gas-to-electric quick guide) gives a pilot-light storage unit as a plain-English example of that "no electricity connection" case — attribute that as distributor guidance explaining the regulation, and ask your plumber to confirm your exact unit.
- Replacement **during a manufacturer, supplier or installer warranty**.

**Transition contracts:** work under a contract entered into with the owner **before 1 March 2027** can still be finished in the **three months after** that date. Directions to fix or rectification orders issued before 1 March 2027 are also carved out. Get contracts dated and signed if you are booking a lawful gas job in that window.

Exemptions are fact-specific. Don't assume one applies because a neighbour's plumber said so — get it in writing from the licensed plumber (and electrician, for switchboard questions) before you rely on it.

## What to replace with: usually a heat pump

For most Victorian houses, the practical electric alternative is a **heat pump hot water system**. VEU and Solar Victoria also recognise **electric-boosted solar** hot water. Heat pumps suit Victoria's cooler climate when you choose a model rated for low outdoor temperatures — see our [cold climate guide](/blog/best-heat-pump-cold-climate) and [sizing guide](/blog/what-size-heat-pump-do-i-need). Placement and neighbour noise matter on tight blocks; read [noise and placement](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-noise-and-placement) before you lock in a spot.

Energy Victoria states that replacing gas hot water with electric saves around **$400 a year**, and that existing all-electric homes save more again when you electrify more of the home. Use that as a government-backed ballpark, then ask your installer to model your tariff and household use.

## Rebates and STCs that cut the upfront cost

Stack these carefully. Order and eligibility differ, and retrospective discounts are often refused.

### 1. Federal STCs (every state)

Eligible air source heat pumps under **425 L** can create Small-scale Technology Certificates. Most households assign them to the installer for an upfront discount. The Clean Energy Regulator caps the STC Clearing House price at **$40 excluding GST** ([buy and sell STCs](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-technology-certificates/buy-and-sell-small-scale-technology-certificates), last updated 14 September 2026). Create certificates within **12 months** of installation if you self-create ([CER solar water heaters and air source heat pumps](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-renewable-energy-systems/solar-water-heaters-and-air-source-heat-pumps), last updated 29 May 2025). See [how STCs work](/blog/how-stcs-work-when-you-replace-your-hot-water).

### 2. Victorian Energy Upgrades (owners and rentals)

Indicative household discounts on the official VEU page (last updated **26 October 2026**):

| Remove | Replace with | Indicative discount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inefficient electric hot water | Heat pump | **$630** |
| Inefficient gas hot water | Heat pump | **$560** |
| Inefficient electric | Solar-boosted hot water | $910 |
| Inefficient gas | Solar-boosted hot water | $700 |

Rules that matter on the same page: the building must be at least **2 years old**, the product needs a **minimum 5-year warranty**, households can upgrade up to **2** systems, private rentals are eligible, and customers must make a **minimum contribution of $200**. Contact an accredited provider **before** you buy — retrospective discounts are typically not possible.

### 3. Solar Victoria hot water rebate (owner-occupiers)

Eligible owner-occupiers can get a **50%** rebate after other discounts (including STCs and VEECs), capped at **$1,000**, or **$1,400** for an eligible [locally made](/blog/australian-made-heat-pump-hot-water) product ([Solar Victoria hot water rebate](https://www.solar.vic.gov.au/hot-water-rebate); page text updated 20 October 2026, rebates-remaining line last updated 2 November 2026, 9:00am). Headline eligibility includes combined owners' taxable income under **$150,000**, property under **$3 million**, system being replaced at least **3 years** old, no prior Solar Homes hot water or battery rebate at that address, and an authorised retailer with an eligible product. Emergency installs are allowed before eligibility is confirmed, but you still have to prove eligibility afterwards.

Solar Victoria and VEU both say combining the two can cut upfront costs by up to **$1,560**, or up to **$1,960** with a locally made product. Solar Victoria also repeats the ~**$400 a year** running-cost saving when switching from gas to efficient electric.

Cross-check amounts on our [rebates by state guide](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-rebates-by-state) and [rebate checker](/rebates) before you accept a quote.

## Timeline: what to do now (late 2026)

1. **Note the age and type of your current system** (storage gas, continuous flow, pilot light or electronic ignition). Take a photo of the compliance plate.
2. **If the tank is near end of life**, get heat pump quotes while you still have hot water. Emergency swaps are stressful and narrow your choices — see our [emergency replacement guide](/blog/emergency-hot-water-replacement-heat-pump).
3. **Ask every quote to show STCs, VEU and Solar Victoria separately**, and to confirm which exemptions (if any) the plumber thinks apply.
4. **Landlords:** line up a licensed installer and keep invoices for minimum-standards records. From 13 October 2026 Victorian rental providers must keep records showing the property meets rental minimum standards.
5. **Don't rush a gas like-for-like** just to beat the date unless you have a clear, documented exemption or a pre-1 March 2027 contract you intend to finish inside the three-month window. For most homes, switching to a heat pump with the discounts above is the cheaper long-term path.
