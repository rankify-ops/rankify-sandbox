Queensland is close to ideal heat pump country. Heat pumps draw heat from the air, and warm air makes their job easy. Whether it's **worth it for you** comes down to what you're replacing, your tariff and the price you pay. Here's how to work it out.

## The climate case

Our local guides use Bureau of Meteorology data. July mean minimums are 10.6 °C at [Brisbane](/heat-pump-hot-water/brisbane), 12.1 °C at the [Gold Coast](/heat-pump-hot-water/gold-coast) Seaway, 9.7 °C at [Sunshine Coast](/heat-pump-hot-water/sunshine-coast) Airport, 13.9 °C at [Townsville](/heat-pump-hot-water/townsville) and 17.2 °C at [Cairns](/heat-pump-hot-water/cairns). Even [Toowoomba](/heat-pump-hot-water/toowoomba), the coolest of these, averages 6.7 °C. That's well inside the rated range of every model in our range, which runs from −5 °C or lower.

## Running costs vs an electric tank

Heating 150 L of water by 45 °C takes about **7.85 kWh** of heat a day.

- An **electric storage tank** turns electricity into heat one for one, so it uses about 7.85 kWh a day, plus the heat the tank loses while it stands (left out here, which flatters the tank).
- A **heat pump** at a cautious seasonal COP of 3.5 uses about **2.24 kWh** a day for the same heat.

That's about **5.6 kWh a day less**, or about 2,050 kWh a year.

### What that's worth on Queensland tariffs

The Queensland Competition Authority (QCA) sets the notified (regulated) prices for regional Queensland. Its 2026–27 prices, which apply from 1 July 2026, are published in the [Queensland Government Gazette of 5 June 2026](http://www.qca.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/050626-29-extra-gazette.pdf). The gazette prices exclude GST; the GST-inclusive figures below are our own calculation (× 1.1).

| Tariff (regional Qld, 2026–27) | Usage price, excl. GST | Incl. GST (our calc.) | Saving per year |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tariff 11, residential flat rate | 26.268c/kWh | about 28.9c/kWh | about $590 |
| Tariff 31, controlled load (supply at least 8 hours a day) | 15.148c/kWh | about 16.7c/kWh | about $340 |
| Tariff 33, controlled load (supply at least 16 hours a day) | 15.240c/kWh | about 16.8c/kWh | about $340 |

The supply hours are the QCA's minimums for customers on the Ergon Energy network, and the gazette notes supply "could be less subject to network operational requirements". In south-east Queensland, prices are set by your retailer's market offer instead, so check the rates on your own bill.

Tariff 31 has the shorter minimum supply window (8 hours a day), so a heat pump on it has less time to reheat the tank. Ask your installer which tariff suits your model and household. If you have rooftop solar, running the heat pump on a midday timer can cost less again.

## What STCs take off

STCs are worked out from your postcode's **heat pump zone** on the [Clean Energy Regulator's](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-renewable-energy-systems/solar-water-heaters-and-air-source-heat-pumps) zone table, the model's count on the CER register, and the 2026 factor of 0.5:

- **Zone 3** (Brisbane 4000, Gold Coast 4217, Sunshine Coast 4558, Toowoomba 4350): about **14–16 STCs** for our range, worth up to **$560–$640** at the $40 Clearing House cap.
- **Zone 1** (Townsville 4810, Cairns 4870): about **12–14 STCs**, worth up to **$480–$560**.

Installers usually sell at the open-market price, which is lower than the cap. Check your exact postcode in our [rebate checker](/rebates).

**State rebate:** we couldn't find a current Queensland Government hot water rebate on the [official home energy savings page](https://www.qld.gov.au/housing/home-energy-savings) when we checked on 17 October 2026.

## The payback sum

Payback in years = (heat pump price after STCs − price of the alternative) ÷ yearly saving.

For example, if a heat pump costs **$1,500 more** than a new electric tank after STCs (a hypothetical figure, so use your own quotes), the savings above pay that back in about **2.5 years** on Tariff 11, or **4.4 years** on Tariff 33. ## When it's less worth it

- **Very low hot water use**, like a single person who's often away. The saving shrinks with usage.
- **A working solar hot water system** that already covers most of your needs.
- **Gas you're happy with**, if you're still on gas for cooking. Run the numbers in our [heat pump vs gas guide](/blog/heat-pump-vs-gas-hot-water-running-costs).
- **Lots of extra work**, like relocating the unit or upgrading the switchboard, which adds to the price.
