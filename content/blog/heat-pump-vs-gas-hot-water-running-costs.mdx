Gas hot water used to be the cheap option. With efficient heat pumps, rooftop solar and the rebates available for switching, that's no longer a given. Here's how to compare the two **using your own bills**, with a worked example you can adjust.

## Why a heat pump uses less energy

A gas water heater burns gas to make heat, and some of that heat goes up the flue or out of the tank. A heat pump **moves heat from the air into the water**, so it delivers several units of heat for each unit of electricity it uses. That ratio is the COP (coefficient of performance).

Manufacturers rate COP at set test conditions. Five of the [six models in our range](/models) are rated from COP 4.5 to about 6.0 at their test conditions, while Stiebel Eltron quotes a seasonal COP of 3.58 for the [WWK 302 H](/models/stiebel-eltron-wwk-302), which is closer to year-round performance. We use a more cautious **seasonal COP of 3.5** in the example below.

## The formula

1. **Heat needed.** Heating 150 L of water by 45 °C (for example, 15 °C to 60 °C) takes about **7.85 kWh, or 28.3 MJ**, of heat a day. That's 150 L × 4.186 kJ/L·°C × 45 °C. Scale it up or down for your household.
2. **Energy bought.** Divide the heat by the system's efficiency: by the COP for a heat pump, or by the efficiency (as a fraction) for gas.
3. **Cost.** Multiply by your tariff: your electricity rate in $/kWh, or your gas rate in $/MJ, from your bills.

## Worked example (example tariffs)

The tariffs below are **examples only**, not current prices in any state. Replace them with the rates on your bills.

| | Heat pump | Gas storage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heat needed (150 L/day, +45 °C) | 7.85 kWh | 28.3 MJ |
| Assumed efficiency | Seasonal COP 3.5 | 70% |
| Energy bought per day | 2.24 kWh | 40.4 MJ |
| Example rate | 30c/kWh | 4c/MJ |
| Cost per day | about $0.67 | about $1.62 |
| **Cost per year** | **about $245** | **about $590** |

On these assumptions the heat pump costs **about $345 a year less to run**, before counting any gas supply charge. A continuous-flow gas heater is more efficient than a storage tank, so the gap is smaller there. Run the numbers with your own system's efficiency.

## Three things that change the answer

- **The gas supply charge.** If hot water is your last gas appliance, removing the gas connection means you no longer pay the daily supply charge on your gas bill. Multiply the daily charge on your bill by 365 to see what that's worth to you. If you still cook or heat with gas, it stays.
- **Rooftop solar.** Running the heat pump on a midday timer uses your own solar power. The real cost of that power is the feed-in tariff you'd otherwise have been paid, which is usually far lower than the grid rate.
- **Your tariff.** Some homes have a cheaper controlled-load (off-peak) rate for hot water. Ask your installer whether a timer or controlled load suits your model and climate.

## Rebates for switching from gas

These are official figures, last checked 16 October 2026:

- **Federal STCs**, Australia-wide. For our six models, a 2026 install earns about **12–18 STCs**, worth up to $480–$720 at the $40 Clearing House cap ([Clean Energy Regulator](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-renewable-energy-systems/solar-water-heaters-and-air-source-heat-pumps)).
- **NSW:** the [Hot Water Upgrade Incentive](https://www.energy.nsw.gov.au/households/grants-rebates/household-energy-saving-upgrades/hot-water-upgrade-incentive) lists an indicative discount of **up to $330** when replacing a gas water heater with a heat pump.
- **Victoria:** [Solar Victoria's hot water rebate](https://www.solar.vic.gov.au/hot-water-rebate) is **up to $1,000** (up to $1,400 for locally made products) for eligible households.

Other states differ. Use our [postcode rebate checker](/rebates), or see the local guides for [Melbourne](/heat-pump-hot-water/melbourne), [Sydney](/heat-pump-hot-water/sydney) and [Canberra](/heat-pump-hot-water/canberra).
