Victoria has the most generous hot water help in the country. Eligible households can stack three discounts on a heat pump: the **Solar Victoria hot water rebate**, a **Victorian Energy Upgrades (VEU)** discount and federal **STCs**. All three usually come straight off your quote. Here's how each one works, using only the official pages, checked on **19 October 2026**.

## The short answer

- **Up to $1,000** from Solar Victoria on an eligible heat pump, or **up to $1,400** if it's on the locally made list.
- It's **50% of the price after other discounts**, so on a cheap system you may get less than the cap.
- You must be an **owner-occupier**, with combined owner income **under $150,000**, a property **under $3 million** and a system **at least 3 years old**.
- Add a **VEU discount** (indicative $630 replacing electric, $560 replacing gas) and **STCs** (about $600–$720 in Melbourne for the models we cover).
- You **apply before you install**, through an authorised retailer, unless it's an emergency.

## How much is the Solar Victoria rebate?

From [Solar Victoria's hot water rebate page](https://www.solar.vic.gov.au/hot-water-rebate) (last updated 12 October 2026):

- Rebates of **up to $1,000** are available for eligible heat pump and solar hot water products.
- Since **1 July 2025**, choosing an eligible **locally made** product lifts the cap to **$1,400**.
- The rebate is **50% of the purchase price**, calculated **after any other discounts** have been applied, including STCs and Victorian Energy Efficiency Certificates.
- It's **paid directly to your retailer** and deducted from your invoice. You pay the rest once the system is installed.

That order matters. Because STCs and the VEU discount come off first, the rebate is half of what's left. On a premium system, half the remaining price is well over the cap, so you get the full $1,000 or $1,400. On a budget system, you might get a bit less (see the worked examples below).

### What "locally made" means

Solar Victoria says a product doesn't need to be 100% made in Australia. "Locally made" means **at least 50% locally made or assembled parts**. The list is published by model number and updated regularly.

## Who's eligible

Solar Victoria says you could receive the rebate if:

- you're the **owner-occupier** of an **existing** property where the system will be installed
- the **combined taxable income of all owners** is less than **$150,000** a year
- the property is worth **under $3 million**
- the address **hasn't already had** a hot water or solar battery rebate under the Solar Homes Program
- the system being replaced is **at least 3 years old** from its purchase date

You must also use an **authorised hot water retailer** and a product on Solar Victoria's **eligible products list**. If you've had a Solar Homes rebate or loan and moved house, you can apply again at your new address, as long as that property hasn't had these rebates before.

**Renting or a landlord?** The Solar Victoria rebate is for owner-occupiers only. The VEU discount, below, is open to private rental properties.

**Solstice Energy customers** affected by the closure of its compressed natural gas network get special exemptions: no $150,000 income cap, systems of any age can be replaced, and a $600 top-up if you don't qualify for a VEU discount. Solar Victoria asks you to call 1300 376 393 for help with these.

## The VEU discount: the second rebate most people miss

The [Victorian Energy Upgrades hot water page](https://www.energy.vic.gov.au/households/victorian-energy-upgrades-for-households/hot-water-systems) (last updated 15 October 2026) lists these indicative discounts:

| You remove | You install | Indicative VEU discount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inefficient electric hot water | Heat pump | **$630** |
| Inefficient gas hot water | Heat pump | **$560** |

A few rules from that page worth knowing:

- The house or building must be **at least 2 years old**, and the product must have at least a **5-year warranty**.
- Only an **accredited provider** can apply the discount, and they should be involved **before** you buy or install. Retrospective discounts are typically not possible.
- You pay a **minimum contribution of $200**.
- Households can upgrade **up to 2** hot water systems, and **private rentals are eligible**.
- VEU is market-based, so the amounts are indicative and not every provider offers hot water upgrades at any given time.

**Combined:** the VEU page says pairing a Solar Homes rebate with a VEU discount can save up to $1,560, rising to $1,960 with a locally made product. Those figures match a gas replacement ($1,000 or $1,400 plus $560). Solar Victoria's page quotes up to $1,630, or $2,030 for locally made, which matches an electric replacement ($1,000 or $1,400 plus $630). STCs come off on top of either.

## Federal STCs in Victoria

STCs apply Australia-wide, as long as the model is on the Clean Energy Regulator's register ([CER](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-renewable-energy-systems/solar-water-heaters-and-air-source-heat-pumps)). [Melbourne](/heat-pump-hot-water/melbourne) and [Geelong](/heat-pump-hot-water/geelong) postcodes are in heat pump **zone 4**, [Ballarat](/heat-pump-hot-water/ballarat) is in **zone 5**, and [Bendigo](/heat-pump-hot-water/bendigo) 3550 is in **zone 3**, so check your own postcode.

For a 2026 install, the register's 10-year figure is halved and rounded down. At the $40 Clearing House cap ([CER](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-technology-certificates/buy-and-sell-small-scale-technology-certificates)), that's worth up to about **$600–$720** in zone 4 for the six models we cover. Installers usually sell STCs on the open market, so the real discount can be a little lower. More on how it works: [STCs when you replace your hot water](/blog/how-stcs-work-when-you-replace-your-hot-water).

## How to apply, step by step

Solar Victoria's process for a planned replacement:

1. **Do some research.** Solar Victoria points you to its Hot Water Buyers Guide.
2. **Talk with an authorised retailer.**
3. **Choose a system** from the eligible products list.
4. **Get a written quote.** The retailer uploads it to the Solar Victoria portal.
5. **Wait for the email** confirming your quote has been uploaded.
6. **Get pre-approval.**
7. **Prove you're eligible** by uploading your paperwork to the portal.
8. **Wait for confirmation** and your **QR code**.
9. **Install within 120 days.** The installer must scan your QR code on site before any work starts.
10. **Pay the difference** to the retailer: the total cost minus the rebate.

### Paperwork you'll need

- Your **quote number** from the authorised retailer.
- **Two forms of ID** with matching names, such as a driver's licence, Medicare card or Australian passport.
- **Proof of income** for every owner on the rates notice or title, all from the same financial year.

Solar Victoria recommends you **don't accept a quote in writing or pay a deposit** until your eligibility is confirmed. If you do pay a deposit, make sure it's fully refundable.

## If your hot water has already broken

You don't have to go cold while you wait. Solar Victoria lets you go ahead with an **emergency installation** first:

1. Contact an authorised retailer and tell them you intend to apply for the rebate.
2. Use only eligible products, and check you meet the eligibility rules.
3. Install the replacement.
4. Apply online as soon as possible, ticking the **emergency installation** box, with your ID and proof of income ready.

If you turn out not to be eligible, you may not get the rebate, so check the rules before you go ahead. Need hot water fast? See our [broken hot water page](/hot-water-broken).

## Replacing gas? The rules change in March 2027

Both official pages say that from **1 March 2027**, when a gas hot water system in an existing Victorian home reaches the end of its life, it must be replaced with an efficient electric system such as a heat pump, not another mains gas heater. The rebates are available now, so if your gas system is getting old, it's worth getting a quote before you're forced to replace it in a hurry. See [heat pump vs gas running costs](/blog/heat-pump-vs-gas-hot-water-running-costs).

## Questions to ask your installer

- Are you a Solar Victoria **authorised hot water retailer**?
- Do you work with a **VEU accredited provider**, and what VEU discount will I get?
- What's the **exact model number**, and is it on the **locally made** list?
- Can you show the **STC, VEU and Solar Victoria amounts as separate lines** on the quote?
- Is any **deposit fully refundable** if my rebate isn't approved?
