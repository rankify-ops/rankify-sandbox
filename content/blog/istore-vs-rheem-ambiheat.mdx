If you want an all-in-one heat pump for a typical family home, two names come up in nearly every quote: the **iStore 270L** and the **Rheem AmbiHeat 270L**. They're the two 270 L units in our range and they're closer than the price gap suggests. Here's how they compare, using each manufacturer's own pages and spec sheets, plus the official rebate pages.

## The short answer

- **Pick the iStore** if you want the lowest price, slightly better efficiency on paper and one simple 5-year parts and labour warranty on the whole unit.
- **Pick the Rheem** if you want an Australian-made unit from a big-name brand, a longer cylinder warranty, and you're in Victoria, where its bigger rebate closes most of the price gap.
- **Either one** suits a household of 3–4 replacing an old electric tank, in any area that doesn't get regular hard frosts.

## Warranty: different shapes, not just different lengths

This is where the two really differ, and it's worth reading your quote closely.

**iStore** ([270L product page](https://heatpumps.istore.net.au/istore-270l-heatpump/), [owner's manual](https://heatpumps.istore.net.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/iStore-owners-manual-270-R290-15-Dec25.pdf)):

- **Full 5 years parts and labour** on the glass-lined tank, the refrigeration system and the electrical parts.
- Applies to units made and installed after 1 October 2022.
- You must **register the warranty within 6 weeks** of installation, and the install must include a 500 kPa pressure reduction valve.

**Rheem AmbiHeat HDc270** ([Rheem product page](https://www.rheem.com.au/rheem/products/Residential/Heat-Pumps/Ambiheat-/Rheem-Ambiheat-HDc270-Heat-Pump-%28with-R513a-refrigerant%29/p/571D270R5)):

- **10 years cylinder supply** and **3 years cylinder labour**.
- **3 years** on the sealed refrigeration system, parts and labour.
- **1 year** on other parts and labour.
- A **5-year whole-of-product warranty**, labour included, when you've received a Solar Victoria rebate, or VEU (Victoria) or ESS (NSW) certificates were created for your install.
- These periods apply to a single-family home.

So the iStore covers everything for 5 years. The Rheem covers the tank for longer but the heat pump parts for less, unless you've used a Victorian or NSW scheme, which most buyers in those states will.

## What they cost after rebates

### Federal STCs (every state)

Both models are on the Clean Energy Regulator's register, so the installer can claim STCs and take them off your price. For a 2026 install, the CER halves the register's 10-year figure (a 5-year deeming period) and rounds down ([CER](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-technology-certificates/calculate-small-scale-technology-certificate-entitlements)). At the $40 Clearing House cap ([CER](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-technology-certificates/buy-and-sell-small-scale-technology-certificates), page last updated 14 September 2026), that's up to:

| Where | iStore 270L | Rheem AmbiHeat 270L |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zone 3: Sydney, Brisbane, Adelaide, Perth | 15 STCs, about $600 | 15 STCs, about $600 |
| Zone 4: Melbourne | 16 STCs, about $640 | 16 STCs, about $640 |
| Zone 5: Canberra, Hobart | 16 STCs, about $640 | 16 STCs, about $640 |

Installers usually sell STCs on the open market, where prices move daily, so the real discount can be a bit lower. Our [compare tool](/compare) works it out for your postcode.

### Victoria: the Rheem's big advantage

Solar Victoria pays 50% of the price after other discounts, up to $1,000, or up to **$1,400 for locally made products**. On the list checked today, Rheem model **571D270** is listed as locally made, and the iStore isn't ([Solar Victoria](https://www.solar.vic.gov.au/hot-water-rebate), page last updated 12 October 2026). You need to be an owner-occupier with combined household taxable income under $150,000, a property under $3 million and a system at least 3 years old, and you must use an authorised retailer.

As a worked example in Melbourne, using our guide prices and assuming you're eligible for the full rebate:

- **iStore:** $3,590 − $640 STCs = $2,950. Half of that is $1,475, capped at $1,000, so about **$1,950**.
- **Rheem:** $4,290 − $640 STCs = $3,650. Half of that is $1,825, capped at $1,400, so about **$2,250**.

That shrinks a $700 gap to about $300. A Victorian Energy Upgrades discount may take off more if your installer is an approved provider. Ask your installer to confirm the exact Rheem model number on your quote is on the locally made list. See [Victorian rebates](/rebates/vic).

### NSW: indicative up to $640

Under the Energy Savings Scheme, the NSW Government lists indicative upfront discounts of up to **$640** when you replace an electric water heater with a heat pump, or up to **$330** when replacing gas ([energy.nsw.gov.au](https://www.energy.nsw.gov.au/households/grants-rebates/household-energy-saving-upgrades/hot-water-upgrade-incentive)). Not every model qualifies or gets the same amount. The system needs at least a 5-year warranty covering the heat pump, tank and other parts, and the installer has to work with an Accredited Certificate Provider. Both units can meet the warranty rule: the iStore through its standard 5-year cover, and the Rheem through its 5-year whole-of-product warranty when ESS certificates are created. Ask your installer to confirm the exact model. See [NSW rebates](/rebates/nsw).

Other states mostly rely on STCs. Check yours in the [rebate checker](/rebates).

## Which one suits your home?

- **Tight budget, anywhere outside Victoria:** the iStore is $700 cheaper on our guide prices and its warranty is simpler.
- **Victoria:** run the numbers with your installer. With the locally made rebate, the Rheem is often only a few hundred dollars more.
- **Queensland and other warm areas:** either one works well. See [is a heat pump worth it in Queensland?](/blog/is-a-heat-pump-worth-it-in-queensland)
- **Frosty mornings** in [Canberra](/heat-pump-hot-water/canberra) or [Hobart](/heat-pump-hot-water/hobart): both are rated to −5 °C and lean on a backup element below that. A CO2 split rated to −10 °C is the better fit. See [best heat pumps for cold climates](/blog/best-heat-pump-cold-climate).
- **Unit near a bedroom or the neighbour's fence:** at about 47 dB, both are all-in-ones, so the whole unit sits in one spot. See [noise and placement](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-noise-and-placement).
- **Bigger household:** if you're 5 or more, check [what size heat pump you need](/blog/what-size-heat-pump-do-i-need) before settling on 270 L.
