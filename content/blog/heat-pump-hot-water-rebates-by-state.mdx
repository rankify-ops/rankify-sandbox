This guide covers what's available in 2026, using **only figures stated on official government pages**. We checked them on **16 October 2026**. Programs change, so always confirm on the linked official page.

## Federal: Small-scale Technology Certificates (STCs)

STCs are available Australia-wide under the federal Small-scale Renewable Energy Scheme. According to the [Clean Energy Regulator](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-renewable-energy-systems/solar-water-heaters-and-air-source-heat-pumps):

- The heat pump must be on the regulator's register of eligible water heaters, and air source heat pumps must be **less than 425 L**
- It must be installed to your state's installer licensing requirements
- The number of certificates depends on the system and where it's installed
- If you assign your STCs to a registered agent, you **may receive an upfront discount**. In practice this is usually handled by your installer
- The scheme runs **until it ends in 2030**

There's no fixed dollar figure, because the certificate count and the market price both vary. Your installer works it out for your exact system.

## Victoria: Solar Victoria Hot Water Rebate

[Solar Victoria](https://www.solar.vic.gov.au/hot-water-rebate) offers a rebate of **up to $1,000** on eligible heat pump and solar hot water products, or **up to $1,400** for products on its locally made list. The rebate is 50% of the purchase price, capped at those amounts. It's paid directly to your authorised retailer and deducted from your invoice.

To be eligible, according to Solar Victoria:

- You own and live in the home where the system is installed
- Combined taxable income of all owners is below $150,000 a year
- The property is valued under $3 million
- The system being replaced is at least 3 years old
- The address hasn't already had a Solar Homes hot water or battery rebate
- You use an authorised retailer and an eligible product

Victoria also runs the **Victorian Energy Upgrades** program, where accredited providers can discount eligible hot water upgrades. We couldn't load the official page when we checked, so we haven't listed an amount. See [energy.vic.gov.au](https://www.energy.vic.gov.au/households/victorian-energy-upgrades-for-households).

## New South Wales: Hot Water Upgrade Incentive

The [NSW Government](https://www.energy.nsw.gov.au/households/grants-rebates/household-energy-saving-upgrades/hot-water-upgrade-incentive) lists indicative discounts of **up to $640** when replacing an electric water heater with a heat pump, and **up to $330** when replacing a gas water heater. It says the final amount depends on factors such as the installer and model.

The incentive is provided as an **upfront discount in your installation quote**. All NSW households and small businesses may be eligible, depending on whether installers in your area offer it. You may not be eligible if you already have an energy efficient system.

## Queensland

When we checked the Queensland Government's [energy and water at home](https://www.qld.gov.au/housing/buying-owning-home/energy-water-home) page, we couldn't find a current state rebate for heat pump hot water. **Federal STCs still apply**, so a Queensland heat pump install can still come with a discount.

## Other states and territories

This guide doesn't cover state programs in the ACT, SA, WA, Tasmania or the NT yet. Federal STCs apply everywhere, and your installer can tell you about anything local.
