If you're replacing an old electric tank, the two most common efficient options are a **heat pump** and **solar hot water**. Both can cut the electricity you use to heat water, and both can attract rebates. They suit different homes, though.

## How each one works

- **Heat pump:** a compressor pulls warmth from the surrounding air and moves it into the water tank, a bit like a reverse air conditioner. It sits on the ground, usually outside, and works day or night.
- **Solar hot water:** collectors on your roof absorb the sun's heat and transfer it to the water. A booster (electric or gas) tops it up when there isn't enough sun.

## Side by side

| Feature | Heat pump | Solar hot water |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Roof space needed | None | Yes, ideally unshaded and north-facing |
| Works at night / cloudy days | Yes | Relies on a booster |
| Noise | Some (37–48 dB(A) in our range) | Very quiet |
| Pairs with rooftop solar panels | Yes, run it on a midday timer | Separate system |
| Federal STCs | Yes, if on the register | Yes, if on the register |
| VIC Solar Victoria rebate | Eligible products, up to $1,000 | Eligible products, up to $1,000 |

## When a heat pump makes more sense

- **Limited or shaded roof space**, or a roof facing the wrong way
- **You already have rooftop solar panels.** A heat pump on a midday timer can use your own power
- **You want a simple swap** where the old tank sits now, with no roof work
- **Apartments and townhouses** where roof access isn't an option

## When solar hot water makes more sense

- **A large, sunny, unshaded roof** facing roughly north
- **Noise is a big concern** and there's nowhere to put a heat pump away from bedrooms
- **You already have a solar hot water setup** and only need to replace part of it
