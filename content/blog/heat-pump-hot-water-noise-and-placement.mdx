Heat pumps are quiet compared with most outdoor equipment, but they do make a steady hum while they run, a bit like the outdoor unit of an air conditioner. Where you put it, and when it runs, decides whether anyone notices.

## How loud are they?

Manufacturers rate noise at 1 metre from the unit. For the [six models in our range](/models):

| System | Type | Rated noise |

| [Sanden Eco Plus 315L](/models/sanden-eco-plus-315) | CO2 split | 37 dB at 1 m |
| [Reclaim Energy CO2 315L](/models/reclaim-energy-co2-315) | CO2 split | 37 dB |
| [Stiebel Eltron WWK 302 H](/models/stiebel-eltron-wwk-302) | All-in-one | 45 dB(A) at 1 m |
| [iStore 270L](/models/istore-270) | All-in-one | 47 dB(A) at 1 m |
| [Rheem AmbiHeat 270L](/models/rheem-ambiheat-270) | All-in-one | 47 dB(A) at 1 m |
| [Emerald All-in-One 220L](/models/emerald-all-in-one-220) | All-in-one | 49 dB(A) at 1 m (45 dB(A) silent mode) |

Two rules of thumb help here. A 10 dB increase sounds roughly twice as loud, so the gap between 37 and 47 dB is noticeable. And in open air, sound from a unit drops by about 6 dB each time you double the distance from it. Compare all the specs in our [side-by-side guide](/best-heat-pump-hot-water-systems).

## The official neighbour noise rules

States set times when residential equipment mustn't be heard inside a neighbour's home. These are from the official pages, checked 16 October 2026:

- **NSW:** heat pump water heaters shouldn't be heard in a neighbour's residential room **before 7 am or after 10 pm on weekdays**, or **before 8 am or after 10 pm on weekends and public holidays**. Councils or police can issue a warning, and fines can follow ([NSW EPA](https://www.epa.nsw.gov.au/Your-environment/Noise/neighbourhood-noise/preventing-neighbourhood-noise)).
- **Victoria:** for home heaters, including hot water systems and heat pumps, the prohibited times are **before 7 am and after 10 pm on weekdays**, and **before 9 am and after 10 pm on weekends and public holidays**. The noise counts as unreasonable if it happens then and can be heard in a habitable room of another home ([EPA Victoria](https://www.epa.vic.gov.au/residential-noise)).
- **Tasmania:** the EPA's guidelines say heat pumps and air conditioners shouldn't be noisier than **45 dB(A) between 7 am and 10 pm, or 40 dB(A) between 10 pm and 7 am**, measured 1 metre from the neighbour's nearest wall ([EPA Tasmania](https://epa.tas.gov.au/environment/noise/noise-publications/heat-pumps-and-air-conditioners)).
- **Other states:** rules are set under each state's environment laws and usually handled by your local council. Check with your council before you install near a boundary.

Noise can also be treated as unreasonable outside those hours if it's excessive, so good placement matters at any time.

## Where to put it

1. **Away from bedrooms, yours and the neighbours'.** EPA Tasmania's guidance is to avoid neighbours' windows, bedrooms and living areas, and to face the back fence or front street, whichever gives the most distance.
2. **Block the line of sight.** Solid fences or garden walls with no gaps screen noise. Hard surfaces like walls and eaves can reflect it, so a tight corner can be louder than an open wall.
3. **Give it room to breathe.** A heat pump draws heat from the air, so it needs the clearances in the manufacturer's installation manual. Boxing it into a cupboard or a narrow, enclosed gap makes it work harder and can make it louder.
4. **Plan for the condensate.** Heat pumps make condensation, so the installer needs somewhere sensible to drain it.
5. **Think about the pipe run.** The closer the unit is to the bathroom and kitchen, the less you wait for hot water.

If the only good spot for the tank is near a bedroom, a **split system** lets the outdoor unit go somewhere quieter. Our [install guide](/blog/how-long-does-heat-pump-installation-take) explains what changes when the unit moves.

## Run it at the right time

A timer is the easiest fix for noise. Running the heat pump in the middle of the day keeps it well clear of the night-time rules, uses your own power if you have solar, and lets it work in warmer air. If you're on an off-peak controlled-load tariff that runs overnight, ask your installer whether that suits your model and your neighbours.
