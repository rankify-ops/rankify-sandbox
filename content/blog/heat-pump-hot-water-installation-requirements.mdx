Short answer: **yes, a heat pump install normally needs both a licensed plumber and a licensed electrician**, and for some split systems a refrigerant-qualified tradie as well. Sometimes one person holds more than one of those licences. Here's who can do what in Queensland, New South Wales and Victoria, the paperwork you should end up with, and the questions to ask before you book. Every rule below was checked against official government pages on **29 October 2026**.

## The short answer

- **Plumbing is always licensed work.** Connecting the water, the relief valve drain and any pressure limiting valve must be done by a licensed plumber.
- **Electrical work needs an electrician**, unless the plumber holds a restricted electrical licence that covers it. Restricted licences don't cover new circuits.
- **Refrigerant pipework needs extra accreditation.** Most integrated (all-in-one) heat pumps and the CO2 splits we quote don't involve it.
- **You should get paperwork:** a Form 4 copy and an electrical certificate in Queensland, a CCEW in NSW, a plumbing compliance certificate in Victoria.
- **Rebates depend on it.** STCs and state discounts require the installation to meet your state's licensing rules.

## What the plumber does

A heat pump is a hot water heater, so the water side is regulated plumbing everywhere in Australia. On a typical swap, the plumber:

- drains and disconnects the old system (a gasfitter's job if it's a gas heater)
- connects the cold and hot water pipes and fits the valves the manufacturer requires
- runs the temperature and pressure relief (TPR) drain line to a compliant point
- tests your mains pressure and fits a pressure limiting valve if needed
- insulates the pipework near the tank.

Those last three aren't optional extras. Building Commission NSW's plumbing advisory note on heat pump installs lists badly terminated TPR drains, missing pressure limiting valves and missing pipe insulation as common faults its inspectors found, all against AS/NZS 3500.4:2021 ([Building Commission NSW: heat pump installations for plumbing](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/compliance-and-regulation/plumbers-and-drainers/standards-and-notes/heat-pump-installations-for-plumbing), last updated 14 March 2025).

## What the electrician does

The electrician connects the heat pump to your switchboard and makes sure the circuit is safe. Building Commission NSW's electrical advisory note says inspectors found heat pump installs without the required 30 mA safety switch (RCD), and that new or upgraded heat pump circuits should use a Type A RCD. It also says a direct-connected water heater needs its own lockable isolating switch next to, but not on, the unit ([Building Commission NSW: heat pump installations for electrical work](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/compliance-and-regulation/electricians/electrical-standards-rules-and-notes/heat-pump-installations-for-electrical-work), last updated 14 March 2025).

Other electrical jobs that can come up:

- **A new circuit** if your old heater was on a circuit that doesn't suit the heat pump's requirements.
- **Tariff and timer wiring** if you want the heat pump on a controlled load like Tariff 31 or 33, or on a timer for solar. See [Tariff 31 vs 33](/blog/tariff-31-vs-33-heat-pump) and [running a heat pump on solar](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-solar-panels).
- **Removing a plug.** iStore's installation manual says the factory 3-pin plug can be removed by an electrician for hard wiring, and an isolating switch is needed.

## When one tradie can do both

In Queensland, restricted electrical work licences cover electrical work incidental to a trade, and WorkSafe Queensland lists "plumbers working on hot water systems" as an example. These licences are subject to the restrictions or conditions printed on them ([WorkSafe Queensland: restricted electrical work licences](https://www.worksafe.qld.gov.au/licensing-and-registrations/electrical-licences/electrical-worker-licences/restricted-electrical-work-licences-permits-other-than-apprentices)). In practice that means a plumber with the right restricted licence may disconnect and reconnect a like-for-like heater, but a new circuit, isolator or switchboard work needs a fully licensed electrician.

Other states have their own licence classes, so the safe question everywhere is simple: **"Who is doing the electrical work, and what licence do they hold?"**

## Split systems and refrigerant licences

This is where people get caught out. Victoria's plumbing regulator spells it out for heat pump water heaters ([BPC Victoria: heat pump installations](https://www.vba.vic.gov.au/plumbing/heat-pumps)):

- **Self-contained unit, or no refrigerant pipework connected:** the plumber needs water supply accreditation.
- **Refrigerant pipework connected on site:** the plumber needs water supply **and** refrigerated air conditioning accreditation.
- **Removing a gas heater:** that's gasfitting work, which needs gasfitting accreditation.

Nationally, the Australian Refrigeration Council licenses refrigerant handling, and its fact sheet on hot water heat pumps says integrated units are sealed systems needing only water and electrical connections.

## The paperwork you should get

### Queensland

Installing or replacing a water heater, including a heat pump, in an existing home is **notifiable work**. The licensed plumber must register a Form 4 with the QBCC within 10 business days of invoicing, and give a copy to the owner or whoever engaged them ([QBCC: what is notifiable work](https://qbcc.qld.gov.au/worksite-building-practice/plumbing-work/what-notifiable-work)). The electrical contractor must give you a certificate of testing after the work ([WorkSafe Queensland: issuing certificates of compliance](https://www.worksafe.qld.gov.au/laws-and-compliance/electrical-safety-laws/issuing-certificates-of-compliance)). You can check an electrical contractor's licence with the [Electrical Safety Office licence search](https://www.electricalsafety.qld.gov.au/electrical-license-search).

### New South Wales

The electrician must lodge a **Certificate of Compliance for Electrical Work (CCEW)** within 7 days of testing, and from 1 July 2026 it must go through the BCNSW eCert portal. Building Commission NSW says this includes like-for-like water heater replacements, and you can get a copy emailed to you if the electrician adds your address ([NSW Government: electrical compliance requirements](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/compliance-and-regulation/electricians/electrical-compliance-requirements), updated 19 October 2026). The NSW hot water discount page says to check your tradie on the [Contractor and Tradespeople Register](https://www.energy.nsw.gov.au/households/grants-rebates/household-energy-saving-upgrades/hot-water-upgrade-incentive).

### Victoria

A licensed plumber must give you a **compliance certificate** when the total job (materials, appliance, labour and GST) is over $750, within five days of finishing, and lodge it with the regulator ([BPC Victoria: compliance certificates](https://www.vba.vic.gov.au/plumbing/renewals-other-requirements/compliance-certificates)). If one plumber removes the gas heater and another installs the heat pump, each issues their own certificate. Plumbers carry a photo ID card listing their licence classes, and you can check them with the regulator's Find a practitioner tool ([BPC Victoria: engaging a plumber](https://www.vba.vic.gov.au/consumers/home-renovation-essentials/engaging-plumber)).

## Why it matters for rebates and warranty

- **STCs:** the Clean Energy Regulator says the system must meet your state's installer licensing requirements to be eligible, and suggests you ask to see the installer's licence on the day ([Clean Energy Regulator: solar water heaters and air source heat pumps](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-renewable-energy-systems/solar-water-heaters-and-air-source-heat-pumps)).
- **Victoria:** BPC Victoria says Solar Homes hot water retailers must use plumbers with the right accreditation.
- **NSW:** the Hot Water Upgrade Incentive says a licensed plumber and/or electrician from your installer does the work, and if you want your own tradie, they need to partner with an Accredited Certificate Provider.
- **Warranty:** manufacturer warranties generally assume a compliant install. See our [warranty comparison](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-warranty-comparison).

## Questions to ask before you book

- Who is doing the plumbing, and what's their licence number?
- Who is doing the electrical work, and is it a full or restricted licence?
- Will I need a new circuit, isolator or safety switch, and is that in the quote?
- Does my model need any refrigerant work on site?
- Will you test my water pressure and fit a pressure limiting valve if needed?
- Which certificates will I get, and when?
- Will you take the STCs and any state discount off the price?
