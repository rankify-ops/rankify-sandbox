Hot water is one of the biggest energy users in the typical Australian home. If you're still running an old electric storage tank, a heat pump is one of the simplest upgrades you can make.

## How a heat pump works

A conventional electric tank heats water with an element, like a giant kettle. A heat pump works more like a reverse air conditioner. It **draws heat from the surrounding air** and transfers it into the water using a refrigerant and compressor.

Because it's moving heat rather than generating it, a heat pump can produce several units of heat for every unit of electricity it uses.

## When switching makes the most sense

- **Your electric tank is 10+ years old** or showing signs of failure
- **You have rooftop solar.** A heat pump with a timer can run in the middle of the day on your own solar power
- **You're moving off gas** as part of electrifying your home
- **You're eligible for rebates** that reduce the upfront cost. Check our [rebate checker](/rebates)

## What to look for

1. **The right tank size** for your household (see our [sizing guide](/blog/what-size-heat-pump-do-i-need))
2. **Noise level**, especially if the unit will sit near a bedroom window
3. **Operating range**, if you live somewhere with frosty winters
4. **Warranty** on both the tank and the compressor
