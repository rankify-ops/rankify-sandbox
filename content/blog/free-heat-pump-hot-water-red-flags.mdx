## Why "free" hot water offers existed

In 2023 and 2024, state energy efficiency schemes in NSW and Victoria paid certificate-based discounts that, combined with federal STCs, could cover most or all of a basic heat pump installed in some homes. That led to a wave of "free", "$0" and "$33" hot water offers, much of it sold by door-knockers and telemarketers.

Regulators have since changed the rules, and they've taken action against businesses that broke them. In March 2026 Victoria's Essential Services Commission (ESC) said one business allegedly staged photos, used banned door-knocking and telemarketing, and paid some consumers cash to use as their co-payment. It cancelled that business's accreditation and banned it from reapplying for five years. The business applied to VCAT for a review, and on 5 June 2026 VCAT granted a temporary stay of the cancellation pending its final hearing ([ESC media release, 27 March 2026](https://www.esc.vic.gov.au/media-centre/businesses-forfeit-42-million-energy-efficiency-certificates-one-banned-false-claims)). The ESC also accepted a court-enforceable undertaking from another provider to fix, at its own cost, heat pump installations at 82 sites where it alleges a contractor made false claims and some upgrades may pose risks to health, safety and property ([ESC media release, 18 March 2026](https://www.esc.vic.gov.au/media-centre/regulator-accepts-court-enforceable-undertaking-over-regional-victorian-energy-upgrades)).

## The rules now, by state

### Victoria

- **You must pay at least $200.** Under the Victorian Energy Upgrades (VEU) program, water heating upgrades installed from 1 February 2025 need a minimum co-payment of $200 including GST per product, and the ESC says the co-payment can't be refunded to you in any form, including money, goods or services ([ESC: VEET Regulations and VEU Specifications changes](https://www.esc.vic.gov.au/victorian-energy-upgrades/updates-reports-reviews-and-data/updates-veu-program/veet-regulations-and-veu-specifications-changes-commencing-25-october-2024)). The Victorian Government's buyers guide says products can't be offered or installed free of charge.
- **No door-knocking or cold calls.** Door-knocking has been banned under VEU since 1 August 2024 and cold-call telemarketing since 1 May 2024, unless you gave prior consent ([ESC: your consumer rights in the VEU program](https://www.esc.vic.gov.au/victorian-energy-upgrades/energy-saving-information-consumers/your-consumer-rights-victorian-energy-upgrades-program)).
- **Five-year warranty.** VEU heat pumps need a warranty against defects of at least five years.
- **Solar Homes rebate on top.** If you're eligible, the [Solar Victoria hot water rebate](https://www.solar.vic.gov.au/hot-water-rebate) is up to $1,000, or up to $1,400 for eligible locally made products, through an authorised retailer. Solar Victoria recommends you don't accept a quote in writing or pay a deposit before it confirms you're eligible, and if you do pay a deposit, make sure it's fully refundable (page updated 20 October 2026). See our [Solar Victoria rebate guide](/blog/solar-victoria-hot-water-rebate).

### New South Wales

- **You must make a minimum payment.** The [Hot Water Upgrade Incentive](https://www.energy.nsw.gov.au/households/grants-rebates/household-energy-saving-upgrades/hot-water-upgrade-incentive) page says you need to make a minimum payment towards the system and installation. Indicative discounts are up to $640 replacing an electric heater with a heat pump and up to $330 replacing gas (page updated 6 February 2026).
- **No unsolicited door-knocking.** IPART says Energy Savings Scheme rules that commenced on 12 September 2025 ban unsolicited door-knocking for all activities ([IPART: new ESS and PDRS rules commenced](https://www.energysustainabilityschemes.nsw.gov.au/news-media/ess-pdrs/new-ess-and-pdrs-rules-commenced)). The NSW Government's [household energy saving upgrades page](https://www.energy.nsw.gov.au/households/grants-rebates/household-energy-saving-upgrades) confirms the ban and a minimum five-year warranty for heat pumps of 700 L or less.
- **Paperwork before work.** You sign a nomination form before installation, and the quote must show the discount and the total cost. After the install, you sign a Post Implementation Declaration and the installer takes photos.

### Queensland and other states

Queensland has no state heat pump discount like NSW or Victoria, so you'll mostly see federal STCs taken off the price. The STC Clearing House price is capped at **$40 excluding GST** ([Clean Energy Regulator](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-technology-certificates/buy-and-sell-small-scale-technology-certificates), updated 14 September 2026). On our guide prices, STCs cover a few hundred dollars, not the whole job. See [Queensland heat pump rebates](/blog/queensland-heat-pump-rebate-2026) and [how STCs work](/blog/how-stcs-work-when-you-replace-your-hot-water).

## Your rights if someone knocks or calls

Everywhere in Australia, the Australian Consumer Law covers unsolicited sales ([ACCC: telemarketing and door-to-door sales](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/buying-products-and-services/telemarketing-and-door-to-door-sales)):

- Door-to-door sellers can only visit 9am to 6pm on weekdays and 9am to 5pm on Saturdays, never on Sundays or public holidays.
- They must say who they are, which business they represent and why they're visiting, and leave straight away if you ask.
- You must get a written sales agreement, and you have **10 business days to cancel** for any reason.
- During the cooling-off period the seller can't supply services or take payment, except for electricity, gas or emergency repairs.
- If the seller breaks the rules, the cooling-off period can extend to 3 or 6 months.

## 9 red flags in a "$0" hot water offer

1. **It's free, $0 or $33.** Genuine NSW and Victorian scheme upgrades need you to pay something. A price that sounds too good usually means the cost is hidden elsewhere, or the offer isn't under the scheme at all.
2. **They knocked or called out of the blue.** Scheme-funded upgrades can't be marketed that way in NSW or Victoria without your consent.
3. **They say they're from the government.** The ESC says VEU businesses must not claim to work for the program, the ESC or the Victorian Government.
4. **They say it's mandatory.** VEU participation is voluntary, and the ESC lists claiming an installation is mandatory as misleading. Victoria's rule that existing homes must replace gas hot water with an efficient electric system from 1 March 2027 applies as the system reaches the end of its life, not because a salesperson turned up.
5. **They offer to refund or cover your co-payment.** In Victoria, the co-payment can't be reimbursed in any form, and the ESC alleged one business paid consumers cash to use as their co-payment.
6. **No brand, model or warranty in writing.** Ask for the exact model number and check the warranty. NSW and Victorian scheme heat pumps need at least a five-year warranty against defects.
7. **No installed price on the quote.** A proper quote shows the system price, each discount (STCs, VEECs or ESCs, Solar Homes) as separate lines, and what you pay.
8. **Pressure to sign today, or install first and "sort the paperwork later".** NSW needs the nomination form signed before work starts, Solar Victoria needs eligibility confirmed before a normal install, and the ESC says businesses must not use high-pressure tactics.
9. **They can't show a licence.** The plumbing and electrical work must be done by licensed tradies. See [who can install a heat pump](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-installation-requirements).

A cheap offer isn't automatically a scam. Some homes still get a low price after stacking discounts. The point is to get everything in writing and check it.

## Questions to ask any installer

- What's the exact brand and model, and is it on the scheme's product list?
- What do I pay in total, and how much is each discount?
- Which accredited provider is creating the certificates?
- Who is doing the plumbing and electrical work, and what are their licence numbers?
- What warranty do I get on the tank, heat pump unit and labour?
- Is the system the right size for my household? See [what size heat pump you need](/blog/what-size-heat-pump-do-i-need).
- Will the old system be properly removed and disposed of?

## Where to report a dodgy offer

- **Victoria:** the [Essential Services Commission](https://www.esc.vic.gov.au/victorian-energy-upgrades/energy-saving-information-consumers/your-consumer-rights-victorian-energy-upgrades-program) takes questions and complaints about VEU businesses.
- **NSW:** IPART for Accredited Certificate Providers, NSW Fair Trading for contract problems, and Building Commission NSW for unlicensed work, as listed on the [NSW household upgrades page](https://www.energy.nsw.gov.au/households/grants-rebates/household-energy-saving-upgrades).
- **Everywhere:** your state or territory fair trading office. The [ACCC](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/buying-products-and-services/telemarketing-and-door-to-door-sales) explains your unsolicited selling rights.
