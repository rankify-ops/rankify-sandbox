## Sydney guide prices before and after STCs

These figures are **supply and standard installation before discounts**, then after **zone 3** STCs (Sydney sits in zone 3 on the Clean Energy Regulator's map). STC values use the Clearing House cap of **$40 excluding GST** per certificate ([CER buy and sell STCs page](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-technology-certificates/buy-and-sell-small-scale-technology-certificates), last updated 14 September 2026). Guide prices and STC counts come from our model data and are provisional until a partner installer quotes your home.

| [iStore 270L](/models/istore-270) | $3,590 | 15 ($600) | **$2,990** |

Air-source heat pumps under **425 L** can create STCs, and if you claim them yourself you have **12 months** from installation ([CER solar water heaters and air source heat pumps](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-renewable-energy-systems/solar-water-heaters-and-air-source-heat-pumps), last updated 29 May 2025). Most installers take the certificates and knock the value off the quote. Open-market STC prices are often a little under the $40 cap, so the STC line on your quote may be slightly less than the table.

## The NSW discount on top of STCs

Sydney has something Brisbane doesn't: a state discount that still exists. The NSW Government's [Hot Water Upgrade incentive](https://www.energy.nsw.gov.au/households/grants-rebates/household-energy-saving-upgrades/hot-water-upgrade-incentive) (part of the Energy Savings Scheme, page updated 6 February 2026, checked 8 November 2026) says:

- The discount is **up to $640** when you replace an **electric** water heater with a heat pump, and **up to $330** when you replace a **gas** water heater with a heat pump.
- It's an **upfront discount on the quote**, not a cash-back you claim later.
- The amounts are **indicative only**. The actual discount depends on the installer, location, model, how complex your home is, admin and compliance costs, and the installer's margin.
- You need to use an **installer who offers the incentive**, and you make a **minimum payment** towards the system and installation yourself.
- If you already have an energy-efficient hot water system, you may not be eligible.
- Check the heat pump comes with a **minimum 5-year warranty** covering the heat pump unit, tank and other manufacturer components.

The page also suggests getting quotes from several installers to find the best deal. It's a separate scheme from federal STCs, so ask each installer to show the **STC discount and the NSW discount as separate lines**.

### Best-case illustration for an electric-to-heat-pump swap

If an installer passed on the full $640 NSW discount as well as STCs at the $40 cap, the iStore 270L in our table would work out to about **$2,350** and the Rheem AmbiHeat 270L to about **$3,050**. That's a best case, not a promise: the NSW page itself says the real discount varies, so treat these as the floor you're unlikely to beat, and compare written quotes.

### What about the bigger "Safeguard Acceleration" discount?

The NSW Government's [hot water discount page](https://www.energy.nsw.gov.au/hotwater-discount) (updated 15 May 2026, checked 8 November 2026) describes larger, limited-time discounts for home owners in listed postcodes, with installs completed by **30 June 2027**. When we reviewed the list it covered **60 postcodes in the Hunter, Central Coast and Illawarra**, not Sydney metro. Full detail is in [NSW heat pump discount postcodes](/blog/nsw-safeguard-acceleration-heat-pump-discount), and all NSW options are in our [NSW rebate guide](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-rebate-nsw-2026).

Check your own postcode in our [rebate checker](/rebates), or see [rebates by state](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-rebates-by-state).

## Rough running costs in Sydney

Sydney retail electricity prices are set by retailers, and they vary by plan, so we haven't printed a single "Sydney rate" here. Instead, use the worked example from our [payback guide](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-payback-period): **150 L a day**, seasonal COP **3.5**, which saves about **2,050 kWh a year** compared with an electric element.

| Your rate (example only) | Yearly saving at ~2,050 kWh |
| --- | --- |
| 33 c/kWh (example general-usage rate) | about **$680** |
| 20 c/kWh (example controlled-load rate) | about **$410** |

Swap in the cents per kWh from your own bill to get your number. If you're on a controlled-load (off-peak) circuit for hot water now, the saving is smaller in dollars, but so is the risk of a big bill.

Plenty of Sydney homes have **gas** hot water. Switching from gas changes the maths: you may be able to drop your gas supply charge if hot water was your only gas appliance, but the NSW discount is lower ($330 rather than $640). See [heat pump vs gas running costs](/blog/heat-pump-vs-gas-hot-water-running-costs) and [vs electric storage](/blog/heat-pump-vs-electric-storage-running-costs).

Got rooftop solar? A daytime timer lets the heat pump run on your own solar instead of grid power. Our guide to [running a heat pump on solar panels](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-solar-panels) explains timers and sizing.

## What drives a Sydney quote up or down

Guide prices assume a simple swap. On real Sydney properties, these are the usual movers:

- **Access.** Terraces, semis and townhouses often have narrow side paths, rear-lane access or courtyards. Getting a tank in and the old one out can take extra labour.
- **Noise and neighbours.** Close boundaries mean the outdoor unit may sit near someone's bedroom window. Ask about placement and timer settings, and read [heat pump noise and placement](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-noise-and-placement). A split system can help where space is tight; see [split vs integrated](/blog/split-vs-integrated-heat-pump).
- **Switching from gas.** Moving from gas to a heat pump usually means a new electrical circuit and capping the old gas line, which a gas-to-electric quote should list.
- **Electrical work.** A dedicated circuit, isolator, RCD or switchboard upgrade adds cost, especially in older homes.
- **Pad or stand.** A level concrete pad or wall bracket may be needed if the old unit sat somewhere unsuitable.
- **Apartments and strata.** Units may need strata approval and may not have outdoor space for an integrated heat pump at all.
- **Tank size.** Couples often suit **270 L**; bigger families more often look at **315 L**. Use [what size heat pump do I need](/blog/what-size-heat-pump-do-i-need).

Sydney's climate is mild enough that cold-weather performance is rarely the deciding factor, unless you're up in the Blue Mountains. If you are, read [the best heat pump for cold climates](/blog/best-heat-pump-cold-climate).

## How to get a real Sydney number

1. Check your postcode in the [rebate checker](/rebates).
2. Pick a tank size from how much hot water your household uses, not just the cheapest model.
3. Ask for a written quote with the model number, STCs, NSW discount, any electrical or gas extras, and warranty as separate lines.
4. Confirm the model is on the CER register and under 425 L if you want STCs, and that the warranty meets the NSW 5-year requirement.
5. Ask whether you'll go on a controlled-load tariff or set a solar timer before the electrician wires it.
