If you own a home in **Canberra** and want to swap your hot water for a heat pump, the main ACT Government help is still a **low-interest loan**, not a rebate. The **Sustainable Household Scheme** lends **$2,000 to $20,000 at 3%** over up to **10 years** with no upfront fees. Concession card holders can get a **50% rebate** on top, and federal **STCs** take a chunk off the price for everyone. One thing has changed since we first wrote this: **ActewAGL's gas-to-heat-pump discount is now concession-only**. Here's how it all works, using only the official ACT pages, first written on 21 October 2026 and **updated on 12 November 2026**.

## The short answer

- **Sustainable Household Scheme:** loans of **$2,000 to $20,000** at **3%**, repaid over up to **10 years**, no establishment or account-keeping fees, no penalty for paying early ([official page](https://www.climatechoices.act.gov.au/policy-programs/sustainable-household-scheme)).
- **Home Energy Support:** concession card holders who own their home can get **50% of the cost back, up to $2,500**, for a heat pump (one Category B rebate; **up to $5,000** if you also claim rooftop solar), plus a **zero-interest loan up to $10,000** ([official page](https://www.climatechoices.act.gov.au/policy-programs/home-energy-support-rebates-for-homeowners)).
- **ActewAGL gas-to-heat-pump offer:** for installations **from 1 November 2026**, **$1,500 off** plus **$400 in bill credits** over three years, **for eligible concession card holders only**, when you replace gas hot water ([ActewAGL terms](https://www.actewagl.com.au/for-home/energy-efficient-homes/heating-and-cooling-upgrade/heating-and-cooling-upgrade-terms-and-conditions)).
- **Access to Electric Program:** a hardship program that can **fully fund** gas-to-electric upgrades, including hot water, by referral only ([official page](https://www.climatechoices.act.gov.au/policy-programs/access-to-electric-program)).
- **Federal STCs:** most Canberra postcodes are in heat pump **zone 5**, where the six models we cover earn about **15 to 17 STCs** for a 2026 install.
- The heat pump must be on the **VEU register**, and the ACT recommends a **zone 5 rated** model that runs to **−5 °C** without the booster.

For Canberra prices and running costs, see our [Canberra cost guide](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-cost-canberra).

## How the Sustainable Household Scheme loan works

The scheme is run by the ACT Government, and **Brighte** is its loan partner. On the [official page](https://www.climatechoices.act.gov.au/policy-programs/sustainable-household-scheme), re-checked 12 November 2026 (the page's own metadata shows it was last modified on 21 September 2026; it doesn't display a last-updated date on the page):

- **Amount:** $2,000 to $20,000 over the life of the scheme. You can put it towards one product or several, such as a heat pump plus insulation or reverse cycle heating.
- **Interest:** **3%** for households (Home Energy Support participants can get a **zero-interest** loan instead, up to $10,000). No establishment fees, no account-keeping fees, and **no penalty for repaying early**. Brighte may charge a fee if you fall behind.
- **Term:** up to **10 years**.
- **Repayments start** once the product is installed and working.
- **Previous participants:** if you applied or were approved before **1 July 2026**, your lifetime limit stays at **$15,000**.
- **Over the limit:** you may be able to borrow more than $20,000 depending on your credit check, but interest may be charged on the extra.

It's a real loan, so you need to meet **standard credit criteria**, including a good credit history and the ability to repay without hardship. Brighte has hardship support if you get into trouble.

### Who can apply

The page says the scheme is open to ACT residents who **own a home** (or hold a licence, for electric vehicle loans only). You must meet the scheme guidelines and Brighte's lending criteria, and **attend a free one-hour workshop**, online or in person.

The ACT Government's [September 2026 household guidelines](https://www.climatechoices.act.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0010/1861570/Sustainable-Household-Scheme-guidelines-for-participants.pdf) add a few points the main page doesn't spell out as clearly:

- For a heat pump (Category B), a participant can be the **owner**, or someone **living with the owner**.
- The property must be in the ACT (including Hall, Tharwa and Oaks Estate, but not Jervis Bay Territory), and can be a **standalone residence or a unit titled property**.
- **Unimproved Value** must be at or below **$750,000** for freestanding homes, townhouses and dual occupancies, or **$300,000** for unit-titled multi-storey apartments (in any year from 2022).
- **New builds aren't eligible**, including a dwelling that received a Certificate of Occupancy or Use in the past 12 months.

For hot water specifically, the [heat pump buyer's guide](https://www.climatechoices.act.gov.au/policy-programs/sustainable-household-scheme/buyers-guides/singing-in-the-shower-a-guide-to-hot-water-heat-pumps) also says:

- You must be **replacing an existing gas or inefficient electric system**.
- Installation needs an **ACT licensed plumber and electrician**.
- If you need a **3-phase power upgrade**, it can be included in the loan.
- If hot water is your last gas appliance, you can then **disconnect the gas** by plugging the line or removing the meter.

## Which heat pumps qualify

The model must be on the [Victorian Energy Upgrades register](https://www.veu-registry.vic.gov.au/Public/ProductRegistrySearch.aspx) under **"Water heater – Heat pump"**. The ACT guide then recommends three checks for Canberra's winters:

1. a model **rated for zone 5** climates
2. **at least 65% energy savings**
3. it can run **on the heat pump alone down to −5 °C**, without relying on the electric booster

That last point matters more than it sounds. A booster is a plain electric element, and the guide warns it can **significantly increase** your running costs if it does the work on frosty mornings. Here's how the six models we cover line up on the manufacturer's rated operating range:

## Rebates and discounts you can use with the loan

### Home Energy Support (concession card holders)

If you hold a **Pensioner Concession Card, DVA Gold Card or Health Care Card**, the [Home Energy Support program](https://www.climatechoices.act.gov.au/policy-programs/home-energy-support-rebates-for-homeowners) can pay **50% of the supply and installation cost, up to $2,500**, for a hot water heat pump. That's **one Category B rebate**. You can also get a separate 50% rebate, up to $2,500, for rooftop solar, for a **maximum of $5,000** per property. If you use the Category B rebate for heating, you can't also use it for hot water, on our reading of the page. A **Commonwealth Seniors Health Card is not eligible** for this program ([Home Energy Support FAQ](https://www.climatechoices.act.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0004/1976593/hesp-homeowners-faq.pdf), October 2025).

You must:

- be a **resident of the ACT**
- own **and live in** the home
- attend a free Everyday Climate Choices workshop
- have an **Unimproved Value** of $750,000 or less for freestanding homes, units and dual occupancies, or $300,000 or less for unit titled multi-storey apartments

The heat pump must be on the [VEU register](https://www.climatechoices.act.gov.au/policy-programs/home-energy-support-rebates-for-homeowners/hot-water-heat-pumps). You can take the rebate on its own (you pay the installer, then claim, and the rebate usually lands in about **10 business days**), or pair it with a **zero-interest loan up to $10,000** through Brighte. Either way, **don't accept a quote until you're pre-approved**. The page is clear on that.

We couldn't confirm whether the 50% is worked out on the price before or after STCs. The official FAQ's worked example uses a **$3,800** heat pump quote, with **$1,900** left after the rebate, which is a straight 50% and sits under the $2,500 cap. It doesn't mention STCs.

### ActewAGL gas-to-heat-pump discount (concession only from 1 November 2026)

This is the section that needed a refresh. ActewAGL still runs an offer under the ACT's Energy Efficiency Improvement Scheme, but the amounts and who can get them have changed.

Its [terms for installations from 1 November 2026](https://www.actewagl.com.au/for-home/energy-efficient-homes/heating-and-cooling-upgrade/heating-and-cooling-upgrade-terms-and-conditions), and the matching [heat pump upgrade page](https://www.actewagl.com.au/for-home/energy-efficient-homes/hot-water-heat-pump-upgrade), say that if you have an **active ActewAGL electricity account** and replace a **gas storage or instantaneous** system with an eligible heat pump, **eligible concession card holders** get:

- **$1,500 off** installation, and
- **$400** credited to your ActewAGL electricity bill over three years ($33.34 a quarter).

There is **no general (non-concession) hot water discount** in those terms. The older terms, still on the same page, covered installations from **1 July to 31 October 2026**: $1,250 off plus $250 in credits, or $2,000 plus $400 for concession holders. That general discount has ended.

A few other conditions from the current terms:

- You need to present an **eligible concession card** whose address matches the installation address. ActewAGL's list is broader than Home Energy Support's and includes, among others, a Commonwealth Health Care Card, Pension Concession Card, **Seniors Health Card**, Low Income Health Care Card, and several DVA cards.
- Households in **financial hardship** without a listed card may still qualify if they're referred by a specified community organisation, or they're on a retailer's hardship program (ActewAGL names **Staying Connected** on the upgrade page).
- The online discount only applies when the quote is requested and accepted through the **Electrify with ActewAGL** platform from a retailer named in the current terms (three retailers are listed for installations from 1 November 2026). ActewAGL nominates which appliances are eligible, and the offer **may be withdrawn without notice**.
- Electric-to-heat-pump swaps aren't covered by this offer. Closing your ActewAGL electricity account generally ends any remaining bill credit.

The ACT Government's Home Energy Support FAQ (October 2025) says you **can** receive a discount from your energy provider and an ACT Government rebate for the **same product**. We only checked ActewAGL's offer, not every retailer's. energy.gov.au's [electric water heater upgrade listing](https://www.energy.gov.au/rebates/energy-efficient-electric-water-heater-upgrade) still showed the old $1,250 plus $250 figures when we checked on 12 November 2026, so use ActewAGL's own terms.

### Access to Electric Program (hardship, referral only)

If you're a homeowner regularly struggling with energy bills, the [Access to Electric Program](https://www.climatechoices.act.gov.au/policy-programs/access-to-electric-program) can **fully fund** replacing all existing gas appliances (heating and cooling, water heating and cooktops) with efficient electric ones, plus ceiling insulation where required. There's **no cost to the homeowner**, and the home is then **disconnected from the gas network**.

It's not a general rebate, and access is by assessment only, through **Care Financial Counselling**. The official [questions and answers](https://www.climatechoices.act.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0003/2794503/access-to-electric-participant-guidelines-questions-and-answers.pdf) say you must own and occupy the home and not own other properties, meet the **National Rental Affordability Scheme income limits**, and live in a standalone house or townhouse in the ACT (apartments aren't eligible). A concession card is **not** required. You also can't combine this program with Home Energy Support or a Sustainable Household Scheme loan. The guidelines mention an Unimproved Value cap but don't publish the dollar figure, and the applicant terms are dated July 2026. Contact Care Financial on 02 6257 1788 or energy@carefcs.org if you think this might apply.

### Federal STCs

STCs work Australia-wide for models on the [Clean Energy Regulator's register](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-renewable-energy-systems/solar-water-heaters-and-air-source-heat-pumps) (page last updated 29 May 2025). Most Canberra postcodes (**2600 to 2617** and **2900 to 2914**) are in heat pump **zone 5** on the CER postcode table. For a 2026 install our six models earn **15 to 17 STCs** there, worth up to **$600 to $680** at the $40 Clearing House cap ([CER buy and sell page](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-technology-certificates/buy-and-sell-small-scale-technology-certificates), last updated 14 September 2026). **Postcode 2618** (Hall and some nearby NSW localities) is **zone 3** and earns a little less. Installers usually sell STCs on the open market, so the real discount is often a bit lower. More: [how STCs work](/blog/how-stcs-work-when-you-replace-your-hot-water).

## How to apply, step by step

1. **Check you're eligible.** Read the household guidelines linked from the [scheme page](https://www.climatechoices.act.gov.au/policy-programs/sustainable-household-scheme).
2. **Book the free one-hour workshop.** It's required before you can get a loan.
3. **Get quotes from Brighte-accredited suppliers.** The ACT guide suggests getting **more than one quote**. Ask each for the exact model number so you can check the VEU register.
4. **Pick your quote.** Your chosen supplier starts the loan process with Brighte.
5. **Get it installed.** Repayments start once the system is installed and running.

If you're a concession card holder, apply for the **Home Energy Support rebate before installation** and wait for pre-approval. If you want both the rebate and the zero-interest loan, the whole thing goes through Brighte. If you also want the ActewAGL discount, ask the installer whether they can quote through Electrify with ActewAGL as well.

## If your hot water has already broken

The loan goes through an accredited supplier, and the rebate needs pre-approval, so an emergency swap can cost you the help if the first plumber you call isn't set up for it. Before anyone starts work, ask whether they're **accredited with Brighte for the Sustainable Household Scheme**. If you need hot water fast, see our [broken hot water page](/hot-water-broken).

## Questions to ask your installer

- Are you **accredited with Brighte** for the Sustainable Household Scheme?
- What's the exact **model number**, and can you show me it's on the **VEU register** for zone 5?
- Does it run to **−5 °C on the heat pump alone**, or does the booster kick in?
- Are the **STCs a separate line** on the quote, and how many does it create in my postcode?
- Does the price include **removing the old system**, any **switchboard or 3-phase work**, and **capping the gas**?
- If I'm eligible for the **ActewAGL** discount, can you quote it through **Electrify with ActewAGL**?
- What's the **warranty** on the heat pump unit and the tank?
