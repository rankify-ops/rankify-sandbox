## 1. Licences and insurance

Heat pump hot water installs involve plumbing and electrical work, and the rules differ by state. Our guide to [how long installation takes](/blog/how-long-does-heat-pump-installation-take) covers the day itself. These questions confirm the right people are doing it.

1. **Who will do the plumbing and who will do the electrical work, and what licence numbers do they hold?** Look the numbers up on the state register. NSW Fair Trading has an [online licence check](https://www.fairtrading.nsw.gov.au/help-centre/online-tools/home-building-licence-check), and in Queensland you can search a register through the [QBCC](https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/).
2. **Are they your own employees or subcontractors?** There's nothing wrong with subcontracting, but you should know who'll turn up and who is responsible if something goes wrong.
3. **Can you show me a certificate of currency for your insurance?** Ask what it covers, and whether it covers damage to your property during the job.
4. **Are you registered for the rebate scheme I want to use?** NSW discounts go through installers partnered with an Accredited Certificate Provider. Victoria's Solar Homes rebate must go through a Solar Victoria authorised retailer. The next section covers both.
5. **Will the licensed person who does the work be on site on the day?** The Clean Energy Regulator tells householders to confirm the installer is the correct person on installation day and to ask to see their licence details ([CER, last updated 29 May 2025](https://www.cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-renewable-energy-systems/solar-water-heaters-and-air-source-heat-pumps)).

## 2. The quote

Ask for the quote in writing so you can compare it with others. The [cost guide](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-cost-australia) explains what typical prices include.

6. **What's the exact brand, model and tank size?** "A 270 L heat pump" isn't enough. You want the model number, so you can check it on the rebate registers and read its datasheet.
7. **Is that price supply and installation, and what's excluded?** Ask about removal and disposal of the old tank, new pipework, a new circuit, a switchboard upgrade, a stand or platform, and access problems such as a roof or tight side passage.
8. **Are STCs and any state discount shown as separate lines?** A clear quote shows the price before discounts, the STC discount, any state discount, and what you pay.
9. **How much would a like-for-like replacement cost?** For a broken tank, ask for a quote for a new electric or gas tank too, so you know the real extra cost. Our [payback guide](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-payback-period) shows how to turn that into years.
10. **How long is the quote valid, and what happens if the price of the model changes?** Ask for the expiry date in writing.

## 3. Rebates and STCs

Rebate rules change, so ask the installer to show you where in the official rules your discount comes from. Our [rebates by state guide](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-rebates-by-state) and [rebate checker](/rebates) help you cross-check.

11. **How many STCs does this model create at my postcode, and what dollar value are you using?** The Clean Energy Regulator caps the STC clearing house price at **$40 ex GST** per certificate ([CER buy and sell STCs page](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-technology-certificates/buy-and-sell-small-scale-technology-certificates), checked 5 November 2026). The number of certificates depends on your climate zone and the install year. See [how STCs work](/blog/how-stcs-work-when-you-replace-your-hot-water).
12. **Is the model eligible?** The Clean Energy Regulator says an air source heat pump water heater must be on its register and have a tank under 425 L to create STCs, and the installer must meet your state's licensing rules.
13. **In NSW: will you handle the Hot Water Upgrade Incentive, and is the nomination form signed before work starts?** The NSW page lists discounts of up to **$640** when replacing electric and up to **$330** when replacing gas, with a minimum five-year warranty ([NSW Climate and Energy Action, updated 6 February 2026](https://www.energy.nsw.gov.au/households/grants-rebates/household-energy-saving-upgrades/hot-water-upgrade-incentive)).
14. **In Victoria: do I meet the Solar Victoria criteria, and will you check before I pay anything?** The rebate is up to **$1,000**, or up to **$1,400** for eligible locally made products, for eligible owner-occupiers ([Solar Victoria, updated 20 October 2026](https://www.solar.vic.gov.au/hot-water-rebate)). Ask about the Victorian Energy Upgrades discount as well, including the minimum co-payment, which is $200 including GST per product under the program's rules.
15. **What happens if a rebate is refused or runs out?** Ask who bears the cost, and get the answer in writing.

## 4. The system itself

16. **Why this model for my household?** A good installer can explain tank size against the number of people, and tariff and timer settings against how you live. See our [sizing guide](/blog/what-size-heat-pump-do-i-need).
17. **Will it work where I live?** If you're in a cold area, ask about the model's operating range and how it handles frosty nights. Our [cold climate guide](/blog/best-heat-pump-cold-climate) has background.
18. **Where will the outdoor unit or tank go, and how noisy is it?** Ask about distance to bedrooms and neighbours. See our [noise and placement guide](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-noise-and-placement).
19. **Is it compatible with my electricity tariff and solar, and what timer settings do you recommend?** Some manufacturers limit third-party solar diverters or controls, so ask before you buy.

## 5. Installation day

20. **How long will the job take, and how long will I be without hot water?** Ask for a time estimate for your own home, and for a plan if the job runs over. See [how long installation takes](/blog/how-long-does-heat-pump-installation-take).
21. **Will you tell me before anything changes, such as a switchboard upgrade?** Extra work should be quoted and approved before it starts, not added to the invoice.

## 6. Warranty and paperwork

22. **What's the warranty, in plain terms?** Ask who covers parts, who covers labour, how long for each, and who you call. The Australian Consumer Law guarantees apply on top of any manufacturer warranty, and the ACCC says you can go to the seller first. Registration windows are short: iStore's install manual says to register within 6 weeks, and Emerald's warranty says within 3 months.
23. **Is there a maintenance requirement?** Some warranties depend on servicing, such as checking the anode or the pressure relief valve. Ask what's required, how often, and who does it.
24. **Which compliance certificates will I get, and when?** In Queensland, replacing a water heater, including a heat pump, is notifiable work, and the licensee must register it with the QBCC within 10 business days of invoicing. NSW electrical work requires a certificate of compliance, and Victorian plumbing work over $750 needs a compliance certificate. Ask for copies.
25. **Who do I call after the install, and how fast will you respond?** Get a phone number and a named contact, not just a generic inbox.

## Red flags during the quoting stage

- Pressure to sign today, or a deposit demanded before a rebate is confirmed.
- A "free" or $0 offer. Read our [guide to $0 hot water offers](/blog/free-heat-pump-hot-water-red-flags) first.
- No model number on the quote, or a model you can't find a datasheet for.
- Reluctance to show licence numbers or insurance.
- A quote that shows only one total with no STC or discount lines.
