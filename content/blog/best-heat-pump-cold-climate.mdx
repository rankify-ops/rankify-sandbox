Canberra, Tasmania, alpine and inland Victoria, and the NSW tablelands all get proper frosts. A heat pump still makes sense there, but **the model you pick matters more** than in milder places. Here's what to look for, using operating ranges taken from manufacturer spec sheets.

## What matters in the cold

1. **The rated minimum temperature.** Compare it with your coldest mornings. CO2 systems in our range are rated lowest.
2. **Defrost.** In frosty conditions the outdoor coil can ice up. Active defrost (as on the Stiebel Eltron) keeps it running efficiently.
3. **A backup element.** Some all-in-ones include an electric element for very cold snaps. Emerald quotes −15 °C with the element on its E models.
4. **Size.** Showers run longer in winter. Size for your household with a little headroom. See our [sizing guide](/blog/what-size-heat-pump-do-i-need).
5. **Placement and noise.** Splits let the compressor sit away from bedrooms, useful when it runs at night in winter.

## Rebates in cold-climate areas

- **Federal STCs** apply everywhere. Canberra (2600) and Hobart (7000) are in heat pump zone 5, and Melbourne (3000) is zone 4. For a 2026 install of the Sanden or Reclaim CO2 systems, that's **17 STCs, worth up to $680** at the $40 Clearing House cap. Open-market prices are usually lower.
- **Victoria:** Solar Victoria's rebate is up to $1,000 (up to $1,400 for locally made products) for eligible households.
- **ACT:** the ACT Government runs Home Energy Support rebates with a hot water heat pump category. We couldn't load the official page to confirm current amounts, so check it directly.
- **Tasmania:** we couldn't confirm a current state program when we checked.

Check your postcode in our [rebate checker](/rebates), or read [rebates by state](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-rebates-by-state).

## Our pick for cold climates

If cold-weather performance and quiet running are the priority, the **CO2 split systems** (Sanden and Reclaim) have the lowest rated operating temperatures in our range. If budget matters more and your winters aren't extreme, an R290 all-in-one like the **Emerald** (rated to −7 °C) is worth a look. Either way, your installer will check your site and recommend one.
