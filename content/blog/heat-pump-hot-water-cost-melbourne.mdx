## Victorian discounts on top of STCs

Melbourne has the most generous stack of hot water incentives of any city we cover. There are two Victorian layers, and they work differently.

### Victorian Energy Upgrades (VEU) discount

The [VEU hot water system discounts page](https://www.energy.vic.gov.au/victorian-energy-upgrades/products/hot-water-system-discounts) (page last updated 26 October 2026, checked 9 November 2026) lists these **indicative** discounts:

| Remove | Replace with | Indicative discount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inefficient electric hot water | Heat pump | **$630** |
| Inefficient gas hot water | Heat pump | **$560** |

Rules on the same page:

- The existing system must be in a house or building that's **at least 2 years old**.
- The heat pump must be an approved product with a **minimum 5-year warranty**.
- Only **accredited providers** (or installers partnered with one) can apply the discount, and it goes **on your quote**.
- Customers make a **minimum contribution of $200**.
- Households can upgrade **up to 2** hot water systems, and **private rentals are eligible**.
- Contact an accredited provider **before** you buy or install. Retrospective discounts are typically not possible.

### Solar Victoria hot water rebate

The [Solar Victoria hot water rebate](https://www.solar.vic.gov.au/hot-water-rebate) (page text updated 20 October 2026; status line last updated 9 November 2026; checked 9 November 2026) is **50% of the price left after other discounts** (including STCs and VEU certificates), capped at **$1,000**, or **$1,400** for an eligible **locally made** product. Solar Victoria lists these eligibility points:

- You're the **owner-occupier** of an existing property.
- Combined taxable income of all owners is **under $150,000** a year.
- The property is valued **under $3 million**.
- The address hasn't already had a Solar Homes **hot water or battery** rebate.
- The system being replaced is **at least 3 years old** from purchase.
- You use an **authorised retailer** and an **eligible product**.

The process runs through the retailer: written quote uploaded to the Solar Victoria portal, you prove eligibility, you receive a QR code, and installation should be done **within 120 days**. Solar Victoria recommends you **don't accept a quote in writing or pay a deposit** before eligibility is confirmed (and if you do pay a deposit, make sure it's fully refundable). If your system has broken down or is unsafe, emergency installs can go ahead first, but you still have to prove eligibility afterwards.

On today's locally made list, **Rheem 571D270** (AmbiHeat 270L), **Reclaim REHP-CO2-315GL-V2** and **Sanden GAUS-315FQTS** appear. The Emerald, iStore and Stiebel models we cover don't, so they're capped at $1,000 if they're on Solar Victoria's main eligible products list. More detail in our [Solar Victoria rebate guide](/blog/solar-victoria-hot-water-rebate) and [Australian made heat pump guide](/blog/australian-made-heat-pump-hot-water).

## The 1 March 2027 gas rule

Lots of Melbourne homes have gas hot water, and that's about to matter. Energy Victoria's [electrification and efficiency standards page](https://www.energy.vic.gov.au/households/electric-and-efficiency-standards-for-buildings) (last updated 9 October 2026, checked 9 November 2026) says end-of-life gas water heaters in **existing homes** must be replaced with electric alternatives **from 1 March 2027**. The VEU page puts it plainly: from that date, if a gas water heater (including a gas pool heater) needs replacing in an existing Victorian home, it **can't be replaced with another mains-gas water heater** and must be replaced with an electric system. Rentals have a matching rule when the system fails and can't be repaired.

A working gas system doesn't have to come out early, and exemptions exist. Full detail is in our guide to [Victoria's gas hot water replacement rule](/blog/victoria-gas-hot-water-replacement-2027). If your gas tank is getting old, pricing a heat pump now, while you still have hot water, gives you more choice than a rushed swap in winter.

## Melbourne winters and model choice

Melbourne's city winters are cool, not icy: the Bureau of Meteorology's Olympic Park station averages a **July minimum of about 7.6 °C**. That's comfortably inside the rated range of every model in our table. Outer and hills suburbs get colder on clear winter mornings, though, and that's where a model rated to lower temperatures earns its price. The Reclaim and Sanden CO2 systems are rated to run at **−10 °C**; the iStore is rated from **−5 °C**. Read [do heat pumps work in winter](/blog/do-heat-pumps-work-in-winter-australia) and [the best heat pump for cold climates](/blog/best-heat-pump-cold-climate), or see our [Melbourne heat pump page](/heat-pump-hot-water/melbourne) for local picks.

## What drives a Melbourne quote up or down

Guide prices assume a simple swap. On real Melbourne properties, these are the usual movers:

- **Switching from gas.** A gas-to-heat-pump job usually needs a new electrical circuit and the old gas line capped. A good quote lists both.
- **Electrical work.** A dedicated circuit, isolator, RCD or switchboard upgrade adds cost, especially in older weatherboards and Edwardians.
- **Access.** Inner-city terraces, narrow side paths and rear-lane access can mean extra labour getting the old tank out and the new one in.
- **Noise and neighbours.** Close boundaries mean the outdoor unit may sit near a bedroom window. Read [heat pump noise and placement](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-noise-and-placement). A split system can help where space is tight; see [split vs integrated](/blog/split-vs-integrated-heat-pump).
- **Pad or stand.** A level concrete pad or wall bracket may be needed.
- **Apartments and townhouses.** Owners corporation approval or a lack of outdoor space can limit options.
- **Tank size.** Couples often suit **220–270 L**; bigger families more often look at **315 L**. Use [what size heat pump do I need](/blog/what-size-heat-pump-do-i-need).

## How to get a real Melbourne number

1. Check your postcode's STC zone and rebates in the [rebate checker](/rebates).
2. If you're an owner-occupier, check the Solar Victoria eligibility points above **before** you accept a quote.
3. Ask for a written quote with the model number, STCs, VEU discount, Solar Victoria rebate, any gas or electrical extras, and warranty as separate lines.
4. Confirm the installer is a Solar Victoria authorised retailer and works with a VEU accredited provider, and that the model is on both eligible lists.
5. Ask whether you'll run it on a timer, off-peak or solar before the electrician wires it.
