## The short answer

- **Pick Reclaim** if you want the lower price and the longest heat pump warranty, and you're happy with a younger Australian brand.
- **Pick Sanden** if you want the brand with the longest history in Australia and a stainless tank as standard, and the higher price doesn't bother you.
- **Either one** is a strong choice for frosty areas, noise-sensitive spots and households of 4–6.

## Warranty: the biggest real difference

This is where the two actually split apart, and it depends on the exact version you're quoted, so check the model number on your quote.

**Reclaim Energy** ([warranty page](https://reclaimenergy.com.au/warranties/)):

- Reclaim Wi-Fi heat pump: **10 years parts and labour**, including the compressor.
- Panasonic heat pump version: **7 years parts and labour**.
- Tank: **15 years parts only** for stainless steel, **10 years parts only** for glass-lined.
- Service call costs are capped at 2 hours' labour including travel up to 25 km; you pay travel beyond that.

**Sanden** ([warranty page](https://www.sanden-hot-water.com.au/warranty-information/)):

- Standard Eco Plus heat pump unit (GAUS-315FQTS): **6 years parts and labour**.
- Heat pump sold with the Sanden Wi-Fi controller (FQTD models): **10 years**.
- Stainless tank: **15 years total**, made up of 10 years at full replacement and 5 more years pro rata.
- Warranty work has to go through an authorised Sanden dealer.

So the cheaper Reclaim often comes with the longer heat pump cover, while Sanden's tank cover is easier to compare because its tanks are all stainless. If you're quoted a glass-lined Reclaim tank, ask whether a stainless one is worth the difference.

## Brand and support

Sanden is a Japanese company that says it has operated in Australia for over 45 years, with Japanese-made heat pumps and 316 marine-grade stainless tanks ([Sanden](https://www.sanden-hot-water.com.au/)). ## What they cost after rebates

### Federal STCs (every state)

Both models are on the Clean Energy Regulator's register, so the installer can claim STCs and take them off your price. For a 2026 install, the CER halves the register's 10-year figure (a 5-year deeming period) and rounds down ([CER](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-technology-certificates/calculate-small-scale-technology-certificate-entitlements)). At the $40 Clearing House cap ([CER](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-technology-certificates/buy-and-sell-small-scale-technology-certificates)), that's up to:

| Where | Reclaim | Sanden |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zone 3: Sydney, Brisbane, Adelaide, Perth | 15 STCs, about $600 | 16 STCs, about $640 |
| Zone 4: Melbourne | 17 STCs, about $680 | 17 STCs, about $680 |
| Zone 5: Canberra, Hobart | 17 STCs, about $680 | 17 STCs, about $680 |

Installers usually sell STCs on the open market, where prices move daily, so the real discount can be a bit lower. Our [compare tool](/compare) works it out for your postcode.

### Victoria: up to $1,400 for both

Solar Victoria pays 50% of the price after other discounts, up to $1,000, or up to **$1,400 for locally made products**. On the list checked today, both the **Reclaim REHP-CO2-315GL-V2** and the **Sanden GAUS-315FQTS** are listed as locally made ([Solar Victoria](https://www.solar.vic.gov.au/hot-water-rebate), page last updated 12 October 2026). You need to be an owner-occupier with combined household taxable income under $150,000, a property under $3 million and a system at least 3 years old, and you must use an authorised retailer.

As a worked example in Melbourne, using our guide prices and assuming you're eligible for the full $1,400: the Reclaim comes to about $6,490 − $680 − $1,400 = **$4,410**, and the Sanden about $7,490 − $680 − $1,400 = **$5,410**. A Victorian Energy Upgrades discount may take off more if your installer is an approved provider. See [Victorian rebates](/rebates/vic).

### NSW: indicative up to $640

Under the Energy Savings Scheme, the NSW Government lists indicative upfront discounts of up to **$640** when you replace an electric water heater with a heat pump, or up to **$330** when replacing gas ([energy.nsw.gov.au](https://www.energy.nsw.gov.au/households/grants-rebates/household-energy-saving-upgrades/hot-water-upgrade-incentive)). Not every model qualifies or gets the same amount, the system needs at least a 5-year warranty, and the installer has to work with an Accredited Certificate Provider. Ask your installer to confirm the exact model. See [NSW rebates](/rebates/nsw).

Other states mostly rely on STCs. Check yours in the [rebate checker](/rebates).
