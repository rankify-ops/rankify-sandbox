## Brisbane guide prices before and after STCs

These figures are **supply and standard installation before rebates**, then after **zone 3** STCs (Brisbane, the Gold Coast and the Sunshine Coast sit in zone 3 on the Clean Energy Regulator's map). Air-source heat pumps under **425 L** can create STCs, and if you claim them yourself you have **12 months** from installation ([CER solar water heaters and air source heat pumps](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-renewable-energy-systems/solar-water-heaters-and-air-source-heat-pumps), last updated 29 May 2025). Most Brisbane installers assign the certificates and take the value off the quote. Open-market STC prices are often a little under the $40 cap, so the line on your quote may be slightly less than the figures above.

## No Queensland household rebate for Brisbane homes

If an ad or a doorknocker still mentions a "$1,000 Queensland heat pump rebate", treat it carefully.

- The **Climate Smart Energy Savers** scheme paid **$1,000** (combined taxable income of $66,667 or less) or **$800** for a heat-pump or solar hot water system. Its regulation set **30 June 2025** as the latest closing date ([Rural and Regional Adjustment (Climate Smart Energy Saver Scheme) Amendment Regulation 2023, SL 2023-0119](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/asmade/sl-2023-0119)).
- Queensland's [home energy savings page](https://www.qld.gov.au/housing/home-energy-savings) listed **no general household hot water rebate** when we checked on **7 November 2026**. What it does list includes Supercharged Solar for Renters (rooftop solar for landlords, not hot water), bill rebates, Solar Bonus, emergency assistance and medical cooling concessions.
- The **Queensland Community Housing Energy Upgrades** program (up to $4,500 ex GST per dwelling) is for **registered community housing providers**, not typical Brisbane owner-occupiers or private landlords.

Full detail: [Queensland heat pump rebate (2026)](/blog/queensland-heat-pump-rebate-2026). State-by-state rebates: [heat pump hot water rebates by state](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-rebates-by-state). Check your own postcode in our [rebate checker](/rebates).

**Bottom line for Brisbane:** federal STCs are the main upfront discount. There is no Brisbane-only rebate to stack on top.

## Rough running costs in Brisbane (Tariff 11 vs 31/33)

Upfront price is only half the story. Brisbane homes on Energex can often put hot water on an economy tariff, which changes the yearly saving.

Using the same worked example as our [payback guide](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-payback-period) — **150 L a day**, seasonal COP **3.5**, about **2,050 kWh a year** saved versus an electric element — and Queensland Competition Authority **2026-27 notified prices** (ex GST; GST-inclusive rates are our ×1.1 calc), last confirmed on **26 October 2026** (the gazette could not be reloaded today):

| Tariff | Price (ex GST) | Price (inc GST, our calc) | Saving at ~2,050 kWh/yr |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tariff 11, general | 26.268 c/kWh | about 28.9 c/kWh | about **$590 a year** |
| Tariff 33, controlled load | 15.240 c/kWh | about 16.8 c/kWh | about **$340 a year** |

Tariff 31 is a similar controlled-load rate with a shorter daily window. For times, meters and which tariff suits a heat pump, see [Tariff 31 vs 33 for heat pump hot water](/blog/tariff-31-vs-33-heat-pump) and [is a heat pump worth it in Queensland?](/blog/is-a-heat-pump-worth-it-in-queensland). South-east Queensland retail rates are set by retailers, so use the cents-per-kWh on your own bill if you are not on a notified Ergon tariff.

If you have rooftop solar, a midday timer can push the real cost of running the heat pump toward the feed-in tariff instead of the retail rate — more on that in our [running costs vs electric](/blog/heat-pump-vs-electric-storage-running-costs) and [vs gas](/blog/heat-pump-vs-gas-hot-water-running-costs) guides.

## What drives a Brisbane quote up or down

Guide prices assume a straightforward swap. On real Brisbane blocks, these are the usual movers:

- **Access and placement.** Narrow side paths, elevated Queenslanders, shared walls and courtyard apartments can mean longer pipe runs, a crane, or relocating the tank. Integrated units need outdoor space and clearance for airflow; splits give more flexibility for the outdoor unit.
- **Noise and neighbours.** Compact lots put outdoor units closer to bedrooms and boundary fences. Ask about setbacks and night-time timer settings — see [heat pump noise and placement](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-noise-and-placement).
- **Electrical work.** A dedicated circuit, isolator, RCD upgrade or switchboard work adds labour. Economy tariffs may need a separate meter or circuit if you do not already have one.
- **Concrete pad or stand.** Many installs need a level pad; soft ground or a raised stand costs more than a pad that is already there.
- **Tank size for the household.** Couples often suit **270 L**; larger families more often look at **315 L**. Undersizing forces more boosts; oversizing costs more up front. Use [what size heat pump do I need](/blog/what-size-heat-pump-do-i-need).
- **Cold-climate kit.** Brisbane winters are mild. Extra cold-climate features that matter in Canberra or the Victorian ranges are usually unnecessary here, which is one reason south-east Queensland is a strong heat-pump climate.

Always ask for **price, STCs and extras as separate lines** on the quote so you can compare like with like.

## How to get a real Brisbane number

1. Confirm your postcode's STC zone in the [rebate checker](/rebates) (most of Brisbane is zone 3).
2. Shortlist tank size from household use, not just the cheapest model.
3. Ask for a written quote with model number, STCs, any electrical extras and warranty in plain lines.
4. Check the model is on the CER register and under 425 L if you want STCs.
5. Decide Tariff 11 vs 31/33 with the installer before they wire it.
