## Canberra guide prices before and after STCs

These figures are **supply and standard installation before discounts**, then after **zone 5** STCs. On the Clean Energy Regulator's postcode table for air source heat pumps, Canberra postcodes **2600 to 2617** and **2900 to 2914** are zone 5. That covers the inner north and south, Belconnen, Woden, Weston Creek, Tuggeranong and Gungahlin, and Queanbeyan across the border. STC values use the Clearing House cap of **$40 excluding GST** per certificate ([CER buy and sell STCs page](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-technology-certificates/buy-and-sell-small-scale-technology-certificates), last updated 14 September 2026). For a 2026 install, each model's 10-year register figure is halved and rounded down. Guide prices and STC counts come from our model data and are provisional until a partner installer quotes your home.

## ACT rebates, loans and discounts on top of STCs

Here's what the official ACT pages list for hot water heat pumps, checked on 12 November 2026. energy.gov.au's [ACT rebates listing](https://www.energy.gov.au/state/act) shows the same programs. The ACT Government pages we used don't show a last-updated date.

### Sustainable Household Scheme (3% loans for home owners)

- **What you get:** a loan of **$2,000 to $20,000** at **3% interest**, repaid over up to **10 years**, with no establishment or account-keeping fees and no penalty for paying early. Hot water heat pumps are an eligible product, and repayments start once the system is installed and working ([Sustainable Household Scheme page](https://www.climatechoices.act.gov.au/policy-programs/sustainable-household-scheme)).
- **Who can get it:** ACT residents who **own their home**, meet the scheme guidelines and the loan provider's (Brighte's) credit checks, and attend a **free one-hour workshop**.
- **Earlier participants:** households that applied or were approved before 1 July 2026 can borrow a maximum of **$15,000** over the life of the scheme.
- **Product rules:** the ACT Government's [hot water heat pump buyer's guide](https://www.climatechoices.act.gov.au/policy-programs/sustainable-household-scheme/buyers-guides/singing-in-the-shower-a-guide-to-hot-water-heat-pumps) says the model must be on the **VEU register under "Water heater – Heat pump"**, and you must be replacing an existing **gas or inefficient electric** system. New builds aren't eligible. It also recommends a model rated for **zone 5** with at least **65% energy savings**, which runs on the heat pump alone down to **−5 °C**. Any **3-phase upgrade** can go in the loan, and you need an ACT-licensed plumber and electrician.
- **How it works:** get quotes from suppliers **accredited with Brighte**. Your chosen supplier starts the loan application.

It's a cheaper loan, not a rebate. More detail in our [ACT Sustainable Household Scheme guide](/blog/act-sustainable-household-scheme-heat-pump).

### Home Energy Support (rebates for concession card holders)

- **What you get:** up to **$5,000** in rebates in total, made up of one rebate for rooftop solar and/or **one rebate of 50% of the supply and installation cost, up to $2,500**, for one of: reverse cycle heating and cooling, **hot water heat pumps** (or evacuated tube solar hot water), electric stove tops and ovens, or ceiling insulation. You may also get a **zero-interest loan of up to $10,000** through the Sustainable Household Scheme ([Home Energy Support page](https://www.climatechoices.act.gov.au/policy-programs/home-energy-support-rebates-for-homeowners)).
- **Who can get it:** ACT residents with a **Pensioner Concession Card, DVA Gold Card or Health Care Card** who **own and live in** the home, attend a free online workshop, and meet the land value limits (unimproved value at or below **$750,000** for freestanding homes and dual occupancies, or **$300,000** for unit-titled multi-storey apartments).
- **Important:** that $2,500 rebate is **one rebate across those product types**. If you plan to use it for heating, you can't also use it for hot water, on our reading of the page.
- **Process:** don't accept a quote until the ACT Government has **pre-approved** your application. On the rebate-only option, you pay the supplier in full, then claim; the rebate usually arrives in about **10 business days**. Or apply for the rebate and loan together through Brighte. The [hot water heat pump page](https://www.climatechoices.act.gov.au/policy-programs/home-energy-support-rebates-for-homeowners/hot-water-heat-pumps) lists the VEU register requirement.

### ActewAGL's gas-to-heat-pump discount (now concession only)

ActewAGL runs appliance offers under the ACT's Energy Efficiency Improvement Scheme. Its [terms for installations from 1 November 2026](https://www.actewagl.com.au/for-home/energy-efficient-homes/heating-and-cooling-upgrade/heating-and-cooling-upgrade-terms-and-conditions) list this for replacing a **gas storage or instantaneous hot water system** with an eligible heat pump:

- **$1,500 off** the installation cost for **eligible concession card holders**, plus **$400** credited to your ActewAGL electricity account over three years ($33.34 a quarter).
- You need an **active ActewAGL electricity account**, and the quote has to be requested and accepted through the **Electrify with ActewAGL online platform**, from one of three retailers named in the terms. ActewAGL nominates which appliances are eligible, and the offer "may be withdrawn without notice".
- Households in financial hardship without a listed card may qualify through a community organisation referral or a retailer's hardship program.

**What's changed:** the same terms show that for installations from **1 July to 31 October 2026**, non-concession customers could get **$1,250 off plus $250** in bill credits for a gas-to-heat-pump swap. That general hot water discount isn't in the terms for installs from 1 November 2026 ([ActewAGL hot water upgrade page](https://www.actewagl.com.au/for-home/energy-efficient-homes/hot-water-heat-pump-upgrade), checked 12 November 2026). We only checked ActewAGL's offer, not every retailer's.

We couldn't confirm whether the ActewAGL discount and a Home Energy Support rebate can be used on the same install. Ask both before you sign.

### Hardship and other help

- **Access to Electric Program:** fully funds swapping **all** gas appliances (including hot water) for efficient electric ones, plus ceiling insulation where needed, for home owners in financial hardship. It's **by referral only**, through Care Financial Counselling, and the home is disconnected from gas afterwards ([Access to Electric page](https://www.climatechoices.act.gov.au/policy-programs/access-to-electric-program)).
- **Household Energy Upgrades Fund:** federal discounted green loans through participating lenders; hot water heat pumps are an eligible upgrade ([energy.gov.au](https://www.energy.gov.au/rebates/household-energy-upgrades-fund)). A loan, not a rebate.

**Watch out for "ACT government" offers.** If someone at your door or on the phone says a heat pump is "government funded", ask exactly which program and check it on the official page. Read our [red flags in "free" hot water offers](/blog/free-heat-pump-hot-water-red-flags) before you sign anything.

## Running costs in Canberra on ActewAGL's published prices

ActewAGL publishes its standard (regulated) ACT prices, set under the ICRC's price direction. These are **ActewAGL Standard plan prices including GST, from 1 July 2026** ([ACT Standard plan electricity prices PDF](https://www.actewagl.com.au/-/media/files/plan-pricing/2026/act-standard-electricity-prices-july-2026.pdf), checked 12 November 2026):

- **Home (flat rate):** 36.9536 c/kWh all day, plus $1.342 a day supply.
- **Home Daytime Economy (time of use):** off-peak **17.6 c/kWh, 11am to 3pm**; shoulder 32.9505 c; peak **49.94 c/kWh, 7am to 9am and 5pm to 9pm**. This is the default plan for new homes and homes with a smart (Type 4) meter.
- **Controlled Load Night:** 27.775 c/kWh. Its eligibility rules are written around element and solar-boosted storage tanks, and we couldn't confirm that a heat pump qualifies, so we haven't used it for the heat pump lines.

Fewer than 20% of ACT customers are on these standing offers, and the ICRC says market offers tend to be cheaper ([ICRC 2026-27 media release](https://www.icrc.act.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0011/3072782/Media-Release-Retail-electricity-price-recalibration-2026-27.pdf)). Treat these as a ceiling, not your exact rate.

Our worked example uses **150 L of hot water a day**. An electric storage tank uses about **2,865 kWh a year** to heat that, and a heat pump with a seasonal COP of **3.5** uses about **820 kWh** (the same base as our [payback guide](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-payback-period)). Because Canberra is cold, we've added a lower COP of **3.0** too, which is what the ACT Government's own cost table assumes.

| Set-up (our calc) | Energy a year | Cost a year |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Electric storage tank on Home | ~2,865 kWh | about **$1,060** |
| Electric storage tank on Controlled Load Night | ~2,865 kWh | about **$795** |
| Heat pump on Home, COP 3.5 | ~820 kWh | about **$300** |
| Heat pump on Home, COP 3.0 (colder case) | ~955 kWh | about **$355** |
| Heat pump on Daytime Economy, heating 11am to 3pm, COP 3.5 | ~820 kWh | about **$145** |

A few Canberra-specific things to know:

- **Coming off a controlled-load tank?** Many older Canberra electric tanks are on an off-peak circuit. On the figures above, your saving is then nearer **$440 to $495 a year** than $755.
- **Colder water, more energy.** Canberra's cold inlet water in winter means both an electric tank and a heat pump use more than our base example. The comparison still holds; the dollar figures move up.
- **Heat in the middle of the day.** The ACT Government suggests a timer so the heat pump runs in the afternoon, when the air is warmer and efficiency is higher. On Daytime Economy, 11am to 3pm is also the cheapest window, and the time rooftop solar is generating. The CER suggests the same timer approach for solar homes.
- **Replacing gas?** The ACT Government's own example, at 2021/22 prices for heating a 300 L tank to 60 °C each day, put gas at about **$850 a year** against **$478** for a heat pump (COP 3), and an electric tank at **$1,435** ([buyer's guide](https://www.climatechoices.act.gov.au/policy-programs/sustainable-household-scheme/buyers-guides/singing-in-the-shower-a-guide-to-hot-water-heat-pumps)). Those prices are dated, so treat them as a guide. If hot water is your last gas appliance, you can also drop the gas supply charge. Compare [heat pump vs gas running costs](/blog/heat-pump-vs-gas-hot-water-running-costs), or [vs electric storage](/blog/heat-pump-vs-electric-storage-running-costs).

## Canberra frosts and model choice

From Bureau of Meteorology data held in our city data, **Canberra Airport** averages a **July minimum of about 0.1 °C**, around **59 days a year at or below 0 °C**, and a **January mean maximum of about 30.0 °C**. Individual frosty mornings are colder than the average. This is where cold-weather ratings matter:

| Model | Rated low end (heat pump) | Canberra fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sanden Eco Plus 315L | −10 °C | Strong; CO2 split built for frost |
| Reclaim Energy CO2 315L | runs "even at −10 °C" (manufacturer) | Strong; quiet CO2 split |
| Emerald All-in-One 220L | −7 °C | Good budget pick for smaller homes |
| iStore 270L, Rheem AmbiHeat 270L, Stiebel Eltron WWK 302 H | −5 °C | Meets the ACT's −5 °C guide; may use a booster element on the coldest mornings |

Ratings are from our model data (manufacturer spec sheets). The ACT Government warns that a **booster element** that kicks in often "can significantly increase your water heating costs", so ask your installer when the booster runs and whether it can be limited. Also check the model's **zone 5** listing and energy saving on the VEU register.

**Noise:** the ACT has legal noise limits at the property boundary that are the home owner's responsibility, so think about where the outdoor unit goes. ## What drives a Canberra quote up or down

Guide prices assume a simple swap. On real Canberra properties, these are the usual movers:

- **Switching from gas.** A gas-to-heat-pump job usually needs a new electrical circuit and the old gas line capped or the meter removed. A good quote lists both.
- **Electrical work.** A dedicated circuit, isolator, RCD, switchboard or 3-phase upgrade adds cost, especially in older homes. Under the Sustainable Household Scheme, a 3-phase upgrade can go in the loan.
- **Model choice for the cold.** A CO2 split costs more upfront but is the safer pick if your spot gets hard frosts.
- **Moving the system.** Shifting the hot water to a new spot means extra plumbing and electrical work.
- **Access.** Narrow side paths, steep blocks and two-storey homes can mean extra labour.
- **Pad or stand.** A level concrete pad or wall bracket may be needed.
- **Tank size.** The ACT guide says standard tanks range from **150–315 L**, with bigger tanks for homes of more than five people. Use [what size heat pump do I need](/blog/what-size-heat-pump-do-i-need).

## How to get a real Canberra number

1. Check your postcode's STC zone in the [rebate checker](/rebates), and see the [ACT rebates page](/rebates/act).
2. If you want the 3% loan or a Home Energy Support rebate, ask the installer if they're **accredited with Brighte**, and get pre-approval before you accept a rebate quote.
3. Ask for a written quote with the model number, the number of STCs and dollar value, any ACT or ActewAGL discount, gas or electrical extras and warranty as separate lines.
4. Ask how the unit performs below −5 °C and when the booster element runs.
5. Tell the installer if you have solar or are on Daytime Economy, so they set the timer for midday heating.
