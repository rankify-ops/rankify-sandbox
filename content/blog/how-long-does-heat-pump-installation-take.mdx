Most heat pump installs are quicker and simpler than people expect. Here's what happens from the moment you ask for a quote to your first hot shower, and what can stretch the timeline.

## Before install day

1. **Quote request.** You tell us your postcode, current system and household size, and a licensed partner installer who covers your area gets in touch. (Our [60-second form](/quote) does this.)
2. **Site check.** The installer confirms where the unit will go, the water and power connections, access, and whether anything extra is needed. Some do this from photos, others visit.
3. **Fixed quote.** You get a fixed price with any rebates you're eligible for taken off, and a date. Anything outside a standard install, like a new slab or a switchboard upgrade, should be in writing before work starts.

## Install day, step by step

Typical timings for a straightforward replacement:

| Step | What happens | Typical time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drain and remove | Water and power are isolated, and the old tank is drained, disconnected and taken away | About 1 hour |
| Position the unit | The heat pump goes on a level base with clearance for airflow and servicing | Under 1 hour |
| Plumbing | Cold in, hot out, relief valve drain, condensate drain and a tempering valve for bathrooms | 1–3 hours |
| Electrical | Connection to a suitable circuit, plus a timer if you're using solar | Under 1 hour, more if a new circuit is needed |
| Commission | Fill, purge air, check for leaks, set the mode and timer, and show you how it works | Under 1 hour |

In total, a like-for-like swap is **typically done in a single visit, around half a day to a day**. Your installer confirms timing for your home.

Hot water to bathrooms generally has to be delivered at no more than 50 °C under the plumbing standard (AS/NZS 3500.4), which is why a tempering valve is part of most installs.

## What can add time

- **Switching from gas.** A licensed gasfitter caps or disconnects the gas line. If the heat pump goes in a different spot, new pipework and a power circuit may be needed. See [heat pump vs gas running costs](/blog/heat-pump-vs-gas-hot-water-running-costs) to decide whether it's worth it.
- **A new location.** Moving the unit away from bedrooms or into better airflow means extra pipework and sometimes a new base.
- **Electrical work.** An old switchboard or no suitable circuit near the unit can mean an extra hour or two, or a separate visit.
- **Access.** Tight side passages, stairs or a unit on a balcony take longer. Measure the space before you choose a model, and ask your installer for the data sheet dimensions (each [model page](/models) links the manufacturer spec sheet).
- **Split systems.** CO2 splits like the [Sanden](/models/sanden-eco-plus-315) and [Reclaim](/models/reclaim-energy-co2-315) have a separate outdoor unit and tank, with pipework between them, so they can take a little longer than an all-in-one.

## After install: first heat-up

A heat pump heats water more gently than a gas burner or a large element, so **heating a full tank from cold can take several hours**, depending on the model and the weather. Many installers commission in the morning so the tank is hot by evening. Systems with a backup element, like the [Rheem AmbiHeat](/models/rheem-ambiheat-270), can use it to boost recovery.

## The paperwork

- **Compliance.** Your plumber (and electrician, if used) provides compliance certificates as required in your state. Keep them with your warranty documents.
- **STCs.** You usually assign your STCs to the installer or their agent, who takes the value off your price. For our range, a 2026 install earns about 12–18 STCs, worth up to $480–$720 at the $40 Clearing House cap ([Clean Energy Regulator](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-renewable-energy-systems/solar-water-heaters-and-air-source-heat-pumps)).
- **State rebates.** [Solar Victoria's rebate](https://www.solar.vic.gov.au/hot-water-rebate) (up to $1,000, or $1,400 for locally made products) is applied by an authorised retailer and deducted from your invoice. The [NSW Hot Water Upgrade Incentive](https://www.energy.nsw.gov.au/households/grants-rebates/household-energy-saving-upgrades/hot-water-upgrade-incentive) (indicative, up to $640 replacing electric or $330 replacing gas) is an upfront discount from participating installers. Check yours in the [rebate checker](/rebates).

## Getting ready

- Clear a path to the old system and the new spot.
- Let the installer know about pets, gates and parking.
- If you have solar, ask for a timer set to run the heat pump in the middle of the day.
- Compare models side by side in our [comparison guide](/best-heat-pump-hot-water-systems) so you know what you want before the site check.
