When you start pricing heat pump hot water, you'll hit two words fast: **integrated** and **split**. An integrated system is one tall unit with everything built in. A split system puts the noisy part in its own box, away from the tank. The right choice comes down to your space, your climate, who's sleeping near it and your budget. Here's how they compare, using manufacturer documents and official pages checked on **23 October 2026**.

## The short answer

- **Pick an integrated (all-in-one) system** if you've got a decent outdoor spot away from bedrooms and want the simplest, cheapest install. In our range they start at a **$3,290** guide price.
- **Pick a split system** if you want the tank inside or tucked against a wall, the heat pump somewhere quieter, or the best cold-weather rating. Our two split models are rated to **−10 °C** at **37 dB**, but start at **$6,490**.
- **Rebates don't pick the winner.** STCs and state discounts depend on the model and your postcode, not the layout.

## How each type works

Solar Victoria's [Hot Water Buyers Guide](https://www.solar.vic.gov.au/solar-hot-water-buyers-guide/section-6-planning-heat-pump-hot-water-system) explains it simply:

- **Integrated:** the evaporator and fan sit on top of or beside the tank, all in one unit. The benefits are simpler installation and a compact footprint. The catch is weight, as integrated units are heavier and may take two people to move.
- **Split:** the heat pump sits in a separate unit that's plumbed to the tank on installation day. That gives you more freedom over where each part goes. For example, the tank can go indoors with the heat pump outside on a wall.

To put numbers on the weight: the integrated [iStore 270L](https://heatpumps.istore.net.au/270l-istore-heat-pump/) weighs **135 kg** and stands **1,956 mm** tall in one piece. The split [Sanden Eco Plus](https://www.sanden-hot-water.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/SANDEN_Brochure_25.pdf) comes as a **48 kg** heat pump unit and a **55 kg** empty 315 L stainless tank, which is easier to carry down a side path or into a laundry.

## Not all split systems are the same

This is the part most comparisons skip, and it matters for who installs it.

The Australian Refrigeration Council's [fact sheet on hot water heat pumps](https://www.arcltd.org.au/media/3nnne5mz/arc5919_factsheet16_web.pdf) describes integrated units as sealed systems that only need water and electrical connections. It describes split systems as having **refrigerant pipes** between the outdoor unit and the tank, which means installation, servicing and removal need a technician trained to handle refrigerant. ARC's [licence types page](https://arctick.org/refrigerant-handling-licence/licence-types) lists a restricted licence (RSS03) that covers installing and decommissioning a two-part hot water heat pump under 18 kW.

Some split systems don't work that way, though. The **Reclaim Energy** and **Sanden** systems in our range join the outdoor unit to the tank with **water pipes**. The CO2 refrigerant stays sealed inside the outdoor unit, and hot water is pumped to the tank:

- Sanden's [brochure](https://www.sanden-hot-water.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/SANDEN_Brochure_25.pdf) says the system needs **installation by a plumber and electrician only**.
- Reclaim's [installation manual](https://reclaimenergy.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Reclaim-Install-Manual-v2-Issue15_June2026_web.pdf) (June 2026) calls for copper water pipe between the heat pump and tank, installed by a licensed plumber or electrician.
- Rinnai also says its split heat pumps use [water line connections rather than refrigerant](https://www.rinnai.com.au/home/discover/integrated-vs-split-heat-pump-hot-water-systems), so they don't need a refrigeration technician.

So if you're quoted a split system, ask whether it uses refrigerant lines or water lines. If it's refrigerant lines, check that whoever does the install holds the right ARC licence.

## Split vs integrated, point by point

### Space and placement

An integrated unit needs one outdoor spot with good airflow and room to service it. iStore says its 270L is [designed for a well-ventilated outdoor location](https://heatpumps.istore.net.au/270l-istore-heat-pump/). If your old electric tank is outside with clear space around it, an integrated unit can often go straight into the same spot.

A split system gives you options when that spot is cramped, inside, or right under a bedroom window. The heat pump has to go outside, but the tank doesn't have to sit next to it:

- **Reclaim:** heat pump outdoors, tank can be indoors in a drained safe tray, with up to **10 m** horizontal and **5 m** vertical between them and no more than 4 bends ([installation manual](https://reclaimenergy.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Reclaim-Install-Manual-v2-Issue15_June2026_web.pdf)).
- **Sanden:** tank up to **15 m** horizontally or **3 m** vertically below the heat pump unit ([brochure](https://www.sanden-hot-water.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/SANDEN_Brochure_25.pdf)).

Longer pipe runs lose more heat, so installers will still keep them as short as they can.

### Noise

Heat pumps run fans and compressors, and Solar Victoria's guide notes noise matters more than with an air conditioner because a hot water heat pump can switch on at any time of the day or night. It suggests putting the unit outside a kitchen or bathroom rather than a bedroom, or using a timer.

In our range, the two splits are rated at **37 dB**, against **45 to 49 dB(A)** for the integrated models. Brands don't all measure noise the same way, so treat small gaps with care, but 8 to 12 dB is a noticeable difference. A split also lets you put the heat pump unit itself away from bedrooms and neighbours, even if the tank has to stay where the old one was. More tips in our [noise and placement guide](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-noise-and-placement).

### Cold weather

Both split CO2 models are rated down to **−10 °C**. The integrated models are rated to **−5 °C** (iStore, Rheem and Stiebel Eltron) or **−7 °C** (Emerald). In most of Queensland, coastal New South Wales, Perth and Adelaide, that difference rarely comes into play. In Canberra, Tasmania, the Victorian high country or the New England tablelands, it's worth paying attention to. See our [best heat pumps for cold climates](/blog/best-heat-pump-cold-climate) and [do heat pumps work in winter?](/blog/do-heat-pumps-work-in-winter-australia).

### Efficiency and recovery

Brands quote efficiency (COP) at different test conditions, so you can't line the numbers up directly. Reclaim quotes COP 6.02 at 32 °C, while iStore quotes 4.8 at 20 °C. A warmer test day gives a higher number.

Recovery claims vary too, and they don't split neatly by type:

- Reclaim's installation manual says a new system gives **50 L of hot water within 20 minutes** and a **full tank in about 3 hours**.
- Sanden claims **up to 50% faster heat recovery** than typical heat pumps.
- iStore claims recovery of **130 L per hour** for its integrated 270L.

If you have a big household or a lot of back-to-back showers, ask your installer about the recovery rate and the tank size together. Our [sizing guide](/blog/what-size-heat-pump-do-i-need) helps with the tank.

### Installation and electrical work

Both types need a licensed plumber for the water connections and a licensed electrician for the electrical work. The differences:

- **Integrated:** one unit, one set of water connections. iStore's [installation manual](https://heatpumps.istore.net.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/istore-install-manual-270L-180L-R290-v1.5.Dec25.pdf) says a unit rated below 10 amps with a 3-pin plug can have the plug removed by the electrician without affecting the warranty, and it still needs an isolating switch.
- **Split:** two parts to place, pipes to run and insulate between them, and a control cable. Sanden lists a **15 amp** circuit, and Reclaim's manual asks for a dedicated **15 amp** circuit for the heat pump plus a separate power point.
- **Refrigerant-line splits** also need a refrigerant-licensed technician, as covered above.

Expect a split install to take a bit longer and cost a bit more in labour. Our [installation day guide](/blog/how-long-does-heat-pump-installation-take) covers what usually happens.

### Price

On our guide prices, the four integrated models run from **$3,290 to $5,690** and the two split models from **$6,490 to $7,490**, before rebates. STCs are similar for both types (14 to 16 in zone 3 for our range), so they don't close the gap. Whether the extra cost of a split is worth it depends on how much the quieter running, colder rating and placement freedom matter in your home. For wider price ranges, see our [heat pump cost guide](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-cost-australia).

### Warranty

Warranty depends on the brand and version, not the layout. For example, Reclaim lists **10 years parts and labour** on its Wi-Fi heat pump unit and **15 years parts only** on a stainless tank ([Reclaim warranties](https://reclaimenergy.com.au/warranties/)). Sanden lists **6 years** on the standard heat pump unit and **15 years** total on its stainless tanks (10 years full replacement, then 5 years pro rata) ([Sanden warranty](https://www.sanden-hot-water.com.au/warranty-information/)). Rheem lists **10 years** cylinder supply and **3 years** on the sealed system for the AmbiHeat 270 ([Rheem](https://www.rheem.com.au/rheem/products/Residential/Heat-Pumps/Ambiheat-/Rheem-Ambiheat-HDc270-Heat-Pump-(with-R513a-refrigerant)/p/571D270R5)). Check the warranty on the exact model number on your quote.

## Rebates are the same for both types

**Federal STCs:** any air source heat pump on the [Clean Energy Regulator's register](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-renewable-energy-systems/solar-water-heaters-and-air-source-heat-pumps) with a tank of **less than 425 L** can earn STCs, split or integrated. The count depends on the model and your zone. The STC Clearing House price is capped at **$40 excluding GST** ([CER](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-technology-certificates/buy-and-sell-small-scale-technology-certificates), last updated 14 September 2026). Installers usually sell for a bit less on the open market. More in [how STCs work](/blog/how-stcs-work-when-you-replace-your-hot-water).

**New South Wales:** the [Hot Water Upgrade Incentive](https://www.energy.nsw.gov.au/households/grants-rebates/household-energy-saving-upgrades/hot-water-upgrade-incentive) gives an indicative discount of up to **$640** when replacing an electric water heater, or up to **$330** when replacing gas. The heat pump needs a minimum **5-year warranty** covering the heat pump unit, tank and other components.

**Victoria:** the [Solar Victoria hot water rebate](https://www.solar.vic.gov.au/hot-water-rebate) (page updated 20 October 2026) is 50% of the price after other discounts, up to **$1,000**, or up to **$1,400** for products on the locally made list. On 23 October 2026 that list included the split **Reclaim REHP-CO2-315GL-V2** and **Sanden GAUS-315FQTS**, and also the integrated **Rheem 571D270**. The [Victorian Energy Upgrades](https://www.energy.vic.gov.au/households/victorian-energy-upgrades-for-households/hot-water-systems) indicative discount is **$630** when replacing an inefficient electric system, or **$560** when replacing gas.

Other states and territories are covered in our [rebates by state guide](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-rebates-by-state), or check your postcode in the [rebate checker](/rebates).

## Which one suits your home?

**An integrated system is usually the better fit if:**

- your current tank is outside with good airflow and some clearance
- the spot isn't right next to a bedroom, or you can run the heat pump on a daytime timer
- you're in a mild or warm climate
- you want the lowest upfront price

**A split system is usually the better fit if:**

- you want or need the tank indoors, in a garage or tight against a wall
- the only tank spot is near bedrooms or a neighbour's window
- you get regular frosts
- access is tight and carrying a 100 kg+ unit in one piece would be hard
- you're happy to pay more for quieter running and a colder rating

## Questions to ask your installer

- Is this system **split or integrated**, and if split, does it use **water lines or refrigerant lines**?
- If it's refrigerant lines, who holds the **ARC refrigerant handling licence**?
- Where will the **heat pump** and the **tank** go, and how long is the pipe run between them?
- Does the quote include a **new circuit**, isolator or switchboard work?
- What's the **noise rating** at 1 m, and can it run on a **timer**?
- How many **STCs** does it earn in my postcode, and what price are you using for them?
- What's the **warranty** on the heat pump unit and the tank, for this exact model number?
