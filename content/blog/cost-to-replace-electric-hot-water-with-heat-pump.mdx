## What can add to the price

Most electric-to-heat-pump swaps are straightforward, but these can add cost:

- **Moving the system** to a new spot, for example away from a bedroom window
- **A new concrete slab or base** if the existing one isn't suitable
- **Switchboard or wiring upgrades**, more common in older homes
- **Extra pipework** or tricky access, like a narrow side passage
- **A tempering valve or other compliance parts** if the old setup didn't have them

A good installer flags these up front, so there are no surprises on the day.

## How rebates bring the price down

Two kinds of incentive can reduce what you pay:

1. **Federal STCs (Small-scale Technology Certificates).** Eligible heat pumps create STCs. Installers usually take their value straight off your quote, so you don't deal with any paperwork. The number of certificates depends on the system and where it's installed, and their value on the certificate market.
2. **State programs.** Victoria's Solar Victoria rebate is up to $1,000 (up to $1,400 for locally made products), and NSW lists indicative discounts of up to $640 when replacing an electric water heater, for eligible homes. We couldn't find a current Queensland program. See our [rebates by state guide](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-rebates-by-state) or check your postcode in the [rebate checker](/rebates).

Because rebates come off the guide price, the amount you pay can be noticeably lower than the figures above.

## Is it worth it?

A heat pump uses far less electricity than a conventional electric storage tank to heat the same water, so running costs typically drop. How quickly the upgrade pays for itself depends on your household size, tariff, climate and the rebates you qualify for. If you have rooftop solar, running the heat pump on a midday timer can also make use of your own power.

## Choosing the right size

Paying for a bigger tank than you need adds cost without benefit, and too small means cold showers. As a rule of thumb, 220 L suits 2–3 people, 270 L suits 3–4, and 300 L+ suits 4 or more. Our [sizing guide](/blog/what-size-heat-pump-do-i-need) goes into more detail, or let your installer recommend one when you request a quote.
