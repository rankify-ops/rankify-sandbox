## Perth guide prices before and after STCs

These figures are **supply and standard installation before discounts**, then after **zone 3** STCs. On the Clean Energy Regulator's postcode map for air source heat pumps, all of metro Perth is zone 3. That covers the CBD, the northern and southern suburbs, the Perth Hills, Rockingham and Mandurah. STC values use the Clearing House cap of **$40 excluding GST** per certificate ([CER buy and sell STCs page](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-technology-certificates/buy-and-sell-small-scale-technology-certificates), last updated 14 September 2026). For a 2026 install, each model's 10-year register figure is halved and rounded down. **Outside Perth:** Bunbury, Busselton, Margaret River, Geraldton and Kalgoorlie are zone 3 too. Albany, Denmark and Esperance are in **zone 4**, which earns a few more certificates (for example 16 for the iStore, 18 for the Emerald). The Pilbara and most of the Kimberley, including Karratha, Port Hedland and Broome, are **zone 2**, which earns a few fewer (12 for the iStore). Check your own postcode in our [rebate checker](/rebates).

Air-source heat pumps under **425 L** can create STCs, and if you claim them yourself you have **12 months** from installation ([CER solar water heaters and air source heat pumps](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-renewable-energy-systems/solar-water-heaters-and-air-source-heat-pumps), last updated 29 May 2025). Most installers take the certificates and knock the value off the quote. Open-market STC prices are often a little under the $40 cap, so the STC line on your quote may be slightly less than the table.

## Is there a WA heat pump hot water rebate?

People search for "heat pump hot water rebate WA how to apply" a lot, so here's the straight answer: **we couldn't find a WA Government rebate for household heat pump hot water.** There's nothing to apply for at state level. Here's what we checked on 11 November 2026, and what each program actually covers.

### What exists in WA (and what it covers)

- **Federal STCs (all of Australia).** On energy.gov.au's [WA rebates listing](https://www.energy.gov.au/state/wa), the Small-scale Renewable Energy Scheme is the program that covers hot water systems. In energy.gov.au's [hot water rebates list](https://www.energy.gov.au/rebate-topic/hot-water), the only WA-tagged entries we found were STCs and a council energy audit kit loan, not a rebate.
- **Energy Ahead (hardship support).** This is the program formerly called the Household Energy Efficiency Scheme. It's a **$13 million** WA Government program to help up to **10,000 households in financial hardship** reduce their bills. Home energy experts work with you, and it "replaces low-efficiency appliances, such as refrigerators, for households in relevant circumstances" ([wa.gov.au Energy Ahead page](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/announcements/energy-ahead-formerly-the-household-energy-efficiency-scheme), last updated 23 June 2026). Anglicare WA delivers it for Synergy customers through the Financial Wellbeing Collective. The page doesn't mention hot water systems, so don't count on it for a heat pump. If you're struggling with bills, though, it's worth a call.
- **WA Residential Battery Scheme.** Rebates and no-interest loans for **home batteries** only ([Energy Policy WA page](https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/energy-policy-wa/wa-residential-battery-scheme), last updated 15 October 2026). It doesn't cover hot water.
- **Concessions.** The WA Government's [concessions and rebates guide](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/multi-step-guides/energy-savings-quick-guide-households/understanding-home-energy-use/government-concessions-allowances-and-rebates) (last updated 12 June 2025) points to bill concessions through Synergy and Horizon Power, Energy Ahead, Concessions WA and the energy.gov.au checker. These help with bills, not with buying a hot water system.
- **Low-cost finance.** The federal **Household Energy Upgrades Fund** works with lenders to offer discounted green loans, and the [energy.gov.au program page](https://www.energy.gov.au/rebates/household-energy-upgrades-fund) lists hot water heat pumps among the upgrades. It's a cheaper loan, not a rebate. Ask a participating lender.
- **Council discounts.** Some Perth councils point residents to discounts through Switch Your Thinking, a local government program. The [City of Canning](https://www.canning.wa.gov.au/residents/environment-and-sustainability/sustainability/our-built-environment/electrify-everything/), for example, suggests checking its heat pump discounts. These are supplier discounts, not a government rebate, and they vary by council.

### What we couldn't confirm

No official WA page says in so many words "there is no hot water rebate". Our answer is based on what the state and federal pages list, not on a written statement. We also didn't find a hot water rebate on the Synergy plan pages we read, but we didn't review every Synergy or Horizon Power retail offer. If a new state program is announced, we'll update this guide.

**Watch out for "WA government rebate" offers.** If someone at your door or on the phone says a heat pump is "government funded" in WA, ask exactly which program, then check it on an official page. The discount you're most likely seeing is federal STCs, which every legitimate installer applies anyway. Read our [red flags in "free" hot water offers](/blog/free-heat-pump-hot-water-red-flags) before you sign anything.

## Running costs in Perth on Synergy's published rates

Most Perth homes buy their power from Synergy, and its standard residential prices are set by the WA Government. That means, unlike in the eastern states, we can use real published rates. These are **Synergy prices including GST, effective 1 July 2026** ([Home Plan A1](https://www.synergy.net.au/Your-home/Energy-plans/Home-Plan-A1); [Midday Saver](https://www.synergy.net.au/Your-home/Energy-plans/Midday-Saver), both checked 11 November 2026):

- **A1 (flat rate):** 33.2621 c/kWh all day, plus 119.2419 c a day supply.
- **Midday Saver (time of use):** super off-peak **8.8520 c/kWh, 9am to 3pm**; off-peak 24.3431 c/kWh, 9pm to 9am; peak **55.3253 c/kWh, 3pm to 9pm**; plus 132.7806 c a day supply. It needs a compatible meter, and a meter fee may apply.

Our worked example uses **150 L of hot water a day**. An electric storage tank uses about **2,865 kWh a year** to heat that. A heat pump with a seasonal COP of **3.5** uses about **820 kWh** (the same figures as our [payback guide](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-payback-period)).

| Set-up (our calc) | Energy a year | Cost a year |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Electric storage tank on A1 | ~2,865 kWh | about **$950** |
| Heat pump on A1 | ~820 kWh | about **$270** |
| Heat pump on Midday Saver, heating 9am to 3pm | ~820 kWh | about **$73** |
| Heat pump running on your own rooftop solar | ~820 kWh | about **$16** in lost export credit |

A few Perth-specific things to know:

- **Solar exports are worth very little at midday.** Under the WA Government's Distributed Energy Buyback Scheme (DEBS), Synergy pays **10 c/kWh** for electricity exported between 3pm and 9pm and only **2 c/kWh** at other times, on up to 50 kWh a day ([Energy Policy WA buyback page](https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/energy-policy-wa/energy-buyback-schemes), last updated 12 December 2024; Synergy's [DEBS page](https://www.synergy.net.au/Your-home/Help-and-advice/DEBS) shows the same rates). Heating water with midday solar is one of the best uses of it. The Clean Energy Regulator itself suggests a timer "to switch on the heat pump during the day while your rooftop solar system is generating". See [running a heat pump on solar panels](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-solar-panels).
- **Keep it out of the 3pm to 9pm peak** on Midday Saver. At 55 c/kWh, a heat pump left to run in the evening costs more than on A1. A timer set for late morning to early afternoon avoids that.
- **Whole-of-home tariff.** Midday Saver applies to everything in the house, not just the hot water. Whether it suits you depends on when you use power overall. Check your Synergy usage data before you switch.
- **The $73 and $16 lines are best cases.** On cloudy days or after heavy use, the tank may top up outside the window. Real costs sit somewhere between those lines and the A1 figure.

Replacing gas? Perth has plenty of mains gas homes. Compare [heat pump vs gas running costs](/blog/heat-pump-vs-gas-hot-water-running-costs), or [vs electric storage](/blog/heat-pump-vs-electric-storage-running-costs).

## Perth summers, mild winters and model choice

From Bureau of Meteorology data held in our city data, the Perth Metro station averages a **July minimum of about 8.1 °C**, frost on about **0.1 days a year**, and a **January mean maximum of about 31.4 °C**. Winter is easy for every model we cover. The things to plan for are at the hot end:

- **Heatwave days.** Rated ranges for our models top out at about **42–43 °C** (iStore, Rheem and Emerald to 43 °C; Stiebel and Sanden to 42 °C). On the hottest days, a unit may lean on its backup element if it has one. Put the unit in shade, with good airflow, away from a hot west-facing wall or a reflective fence.
- **Coastal air.** Homes close to the beach get salty air. Ask the installer about placement and what the warranty says about coastal installs.
- **Model choice.** Because Perth's winters are mild, you don't need to pay extra for cold-climate performance. Value integrated units like the iStore or Rheem suit most family homes. The CO2 splits (Reclaim, Sanden) earn their keep through quietness, a split layout where space is tight, and long tank warranties rather than the cold.

See our [Perth heat pump page](/heat-pump-hot-water/perth) for local picks, and [the best heat pump for cold climates](/blog/best-heat-pump-cold-climate) if you're in the colder South West or Great Southern.

## What drives a Perth quote up or down

Guide prices assume a simple swap. On real Perth properties, these are the usual movers:

- **Switching from gas.** A gas-to-heat-pump job usually needs a new electrical circuit and the old gas line capped. A good quote lists both.
- **Electrical work.** A dedicated circuit, isolator, RCD or switchboard upgrade adds cost, especially in older homes.
- **Moving the system.** Shifting the hot water to a new spot means extra plumbing and electrical work.
- **Access.** Narrow side paths, high fences and two-storey homes can mean extra labour.
- **Noise and neighbours.** Small blocks mean the outdoor unit may sit near a bedroom window. Read [heat pump noise and placement](/blog/heat-pump-hot-water-noise-and-placement). A split system can help where space is tight; see [split vs integrated](/blog/split-vs-integrated-heat-pump).
- **Pad or stand.** A level concrete pad or wall bracket may be needed.
- **Tank size.** Couples often suit **220–270 L**; bigger families more often look at **315 L**. Use [what size heat pump do I need](/blog/what-size-heat-pump-do-i-need).
