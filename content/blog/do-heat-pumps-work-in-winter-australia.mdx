It's the most common question we hear from homes south of the Murray and up in the tablelands: **will a heat pump still give me hot showers in July?** For most Australian homes, the short answer is yes, as long as you choose the right system and it's installed well.

## How a heat pump heats water when it's cold outside

A heat pump doesn't make heat from scratch. It pulls warmth out of the surrounding air with a refrigerant, concentrates it with a compressor, and moves it into your water tank. Even cold air holds usable heat, so this keeps working well below the temperatures most Australian cities see in winter.

What changes in the cold is **efficiency and recovery time**:

- The colder the air, the harder the compressor works to reach your set temperature
- The tank can take longer to reheat after a run of showers
- In frosty conditions the unit may briefly **defrost** its outdoor coil, which is normal

## Which parts of Australia need a cold-climate model?

Most coastal homes in Queensland, New South Wales and the warmer parts of Victoria rarely see the kind of cold that troubles a standard heat pump. Homes that regularly get **frost or sub-zero mornings** should look harder at the specs. These include Canberra, Tasmania, alpine and inland Victoria, and the NSW tablelands.

For those areas, a **cold-climate rated** system is designed to keep running efficiently in lower temperatures. Examples include the CO2 split systems in our range, like the [Reclaim Energy CO2 315L](/models/reclaim-energy-co2-315) and [Sanden Eco Plus 315L](/models/sanden-eco-plus-315).

## Five ways to get the best winter performance

1. **Size it right.** A tank that's too small for your household works harder in winter, when showers run longer. Our [sizing guide](/blog/what-size-heat-pump-do-i-need) has a quick rule of thumb.
2. **Pick the right spot.** Good airflow matters. A sheltered spot that still gets air movement usually beats a cramped corner, and your installer will check clearances.
3. **Use a timer.** Running the heat pump during the warmest part of the day helps efficiency. If you have rooftop solar, a midday timer can also use your own power.
4. **Consider a split system** if space or noise is a concern. The compressor sits apart from the tank, giving more placement options.
5. **Keep it maintained.** Clear leaves and debris from around the unit and follow the manufacturer's service schedule.

## Will my running costs go up in winter?

Usually a little, because the system works harder and you tend to use more hot water. Heat pumps are still typically far more efficient than a conventional electric storage tank year-round. Your actual cost depends on your tariff, usage and climate. Your installer can estimate it for your home.
